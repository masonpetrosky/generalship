## Decisions and limits

- **All three opening strengths remain unknown.** Iuka's frozen fields give
  approx. 4,000–4,500 US and 3,200 CS; NPS says Price had about 14,000; Greene gives
  Rosecrans's column about 9,000, Ord's about 6,000, Hamilton's 2,800 in action and
  two Confederate brigades of three to four thousand. Corinth's live 23,000 / 22,000
  agrees with Greene's pre-battle returns, which may be a shared dependence rather
  than corroboration. Hatchie's Bridge has no counts, and the sources differ on
  whether Hurlbut brought a brigade or a division.
- **Casualty disagreements remain** (frozen/live): Iuka **1,482 / 1,384**, Corinth
  **7,197 / 7,150**; Hatchie's Bridge agrees at 900. Greene's Iuka figures include
  Price's reported 496 against a provost-marshal tally of 1,438 with prisoners and an
  estimate. His Corinth figure of over nine thousand is an inference from an assumed
  wounded-to-killed ratio. None is adopted.
- **Information:** Greene attributes Grant's failure to hear the Iuka firing to the
  wind and a late-arriving message; NPS reports Ord's and Grant's statements that
  they heard nothing. Neither is verified acoustically or by a message log.
- **Command roles:** why the Fulton road was left open is disputed (Rosecrans's
  stated fear of dividing his force, per NPS, against Greene's account that he was
  forced into action). Van Dorn commanded as senior; Hébert's sickness and two
  eccentric Union advances are attributed judgments. Ord and Hurlbut held command
  in turn at Hatchie's Bridge; Ord's live heading rank is not adopted. Rosecrans's
  delayed pursuit is post-outcome and not added to Corinth's result.
- Corinth's fortification lines are tagged `inherited`. The three intervals share
  troops; no additive campaign credit is implied.
