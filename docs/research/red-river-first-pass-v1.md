## Inspection and stopping record

Read all seven frozen battle, force and commander row sets and the seven retained NPS pages in
full. NPS/CWSAC and the Arnold tables are one family. Every record uses the same two further
families.

- **Irwin, *History of the Nineteenth Army Corps*** (Putnam's, 1892; Allen County Public Library
  scan `historyofninetee00irwi`), newly pinned with catalog metadata and full OCR. Irwin was the
  assistant adjutant-general of the corps and of Banks's Department of the Gulf, so this is an
  interested Union history. It cites the *Official Records* and gives Taylor's and Kirby Smith's
  figures, so it is not independent of them. Selected:
  - the title page;
  - Chapter XXIII, The Red River, including Fort De Russy (OCR pp.282–298; the p.287 head reads
    "2.Z7");
  - Chapter XXIV, Sabine Cross-Roads (pp.299–312; the p.309 head reads "3(X)");
  - Chapter XXV, Pleasant Hill (pp.313–322);
  - Chapter XXVI, Grand Ecore, including Blair's Landing (pp.323–327);
  - Chapter XXVII, The Crossing of Cane River, to the arrival at Alexandria (pp.328–334);
  - Chapter XXIX, Last Days in Louisiana, including Mansura and Yellow Bayou, to the camp at
    Morganza (pp.344–348);
  - the appendix loss table and special field returns for Sabine Cross-Roads and Pleasant Hill
    (pp.472–475), whose columns are garbled in OCR.
- **Taylor's** reports, letters, dispatches and order (*Official Records* Series I, Volume XXXIV,
  Part 1, No. 95), newly pinned from the University of Illinois scan `warofrebellion341unit`
  with catalog metadata and full OCR. Before use, the catalog volume ("v.34:1") and the OCR title
  ("SERIES I— VOLUME XXXIV — IN FOUR PARTS. PART I— REPORTS.", 1891 imprint) were checked. All of
  No. 95 (pp.560–597) was read. Selected:
  - the July 31 transmittal and the April 18 report (pp.560–572);
  - his 10 p.m. letters of March 13 to Douglas and Walker and his March 14 letter (Inclosure D)
    on Fort De Russy (pp.575–578);
  - Inclosure F, the list of ordnance lost at Fort De Russy signed by his chief of artillery
    (pp.578–579);
  - the two April 24 dispatches on Monett's Ferry (pp.579–581);
  - the May 10 dispatch (pp.589–591);
  - the second May 14 dispatch and those of May 16, 18 and 19 (pp.592–595);
  - the General Orders of April 11 (p.596).

## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.**
  - Context only:
    - the Simsport landing and Walker's withdrawal (March 12–13);
    - Henderson's Hill (March 21);
    - the April 7 cavalry fight at Wilson's farm;
    - Liddell's operations on the east bank and the fleet's passage to Grand Ecore
      (April 14–15);
    - Wharton's attacks near Cloutierville (April 22–24);
    - the Alexandria dam and the May 1–13 river fighting;
    - the Marksville fight (May 15);
    - the Moreauville attacks (May 17);
    - the Atchafalaya crossing (May 19–20).
  - The April 13 firing at Bouledeau Point and Campti lies within Blair's Landing's frozen
    interval but is recorded separately.
  - The frozen campaign label and the live date field read March–April 1864. Mansura and Yellow
    Bayou fall in May; their own frozen dates stand.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The only frozen bound is Fort DeRussy's Confederate 350,
  the NPS garrison figure; no inspected passage gives its basis. Every other bound is blank. Every
  figure is recorded with its date and scope, and none is adopted:
  - Irwin, end of March: 9,000 for duty under A. J. Smith.
  - Irwin, Mansfield:
    - Ransom's 2,400 and Cameron's 1,300;
    - a Union fighting line below 3,500 and a force at hand under 5,000, against nearly 10,000;
    - Emory's five thousand;
    - Taylor's "at least sixteen thousand", including divisions not on the field.
  - Taylor, Mansfield: Churchill and Parsons about 4,000 bayonets.
  - Pleasant Hill:
    - Taylor: 12,000 against twenty-odd thousand;
    - Irwin: the Union 11,000–13,000; Kirby Smith's figures account for 16,000 Confederates.
  - Monett's Ferry:
    - Taylor: Banks some 15,000;
    - Irwin: Polignac 2,000 muskets besides the cavalry.
  - Mansura:
    - Taylor: probably 16,000 Union on the field; "four times their number" (May 14); his own
      "scarce 6,000" (May 10).
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - Fort DeRussy losses: Irwin's 3 killed and 35 wounded and 317 captured, against Taylor's
    4 and 30, 5 and 4, and 200 claimed prisoners. The frozen and live totals agree (317).
  - Mansfield:
    - frozen 4,400 against live 3,235;
    - Irwin's Union 2,186 and Taylor's 1,000 (reported by Irwin);
    - Taylor's claimed 2,500 prisoners and twenty-one guns against Irwin's twenty guns;
    - the evening at the creek;
    - who ordered Mouton's attack.
  - Pleasant Hill:
    - frozen 3,100 against live 2,995;
    - Irwin's Union 1,506 against the frozen US 1,100;
    - Taylor's 1,500;
    - Taylor's claim of victory against Kirby Smith's "finally repulsed".
  - Blair's Landing:
    - frozen 207 against live 117;
    - Irwin's 19 for the whole voyage;
    - thirty vessels (Taylor) against six gunboats and twenty transports (Irwin).
  - Monett's Ferry: three or four batteries and Terrell's presence. Birge's loss of about 200 is
    one column's.
  - Mansura: no figure in any inspected passage; the frozen text reads Unknown and the live field
    zero.
  - Yellow Bayou:
    - Mower's 267 against the frozen US 360;
    - Taylor's 500 against the Confederate returns' 452 killed and wounded (per Irwin);
    - Taylor's identification of the Union troops as fresh troops from Texas, against the
      Sixteenth Corps named by Irwin and the frozen record.
- **Two-day totals.** Taylor's 2,500 and Irwin's combined appendix return cover April 8 and 9
  together. They are cited in both LA018 and LA019 and are not split or added.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live page gives A. J. Smith as Major General at Fort DeRussy (frozen Brigadier General)
    and does not list Mower.
  - Taylor styles Green Major General; the frozen and live records give Brigadier General.
  - At Monett's Ferry, direction passed from Banks to Franklin to Emory.
  - At Yellow Bayou, Mower commanded in A. J. Smith's absence.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit, and live ranks are not adopted.
- **Tags.** Taylor chose the Mansfield ground and Bee the Monett's Ferry position, so neither is
  tagged `inherited`. Fort De Russy's works predate the attack, but their state on March 14 is
  unestablished. All claims stay `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Campaign contribution.** The following are recorded as campaign context, not tactical
  outcome:
  - the opening of the river to Alexandria;
  - Banks's retreat after Pleasant Hill;
  - Steele's abandonment of the junction;
  - the Army of the Tennessee's escape as an army.

  NPS's missed-opportunity statements are counterfactual judgments.
- **Families.** Irwin is placed in `irwin-nineteenth-corps-1892` and Taylor in
  `taylor-red-river-1864-reports`. Neither author had an earlier registry group. The OR parent is
  in `or-series-i-volume-xxxiv`.

The seven null unknowns are the seven opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability,
causal effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The cohort, both admission proposals and the
baseline are unchanged, with **zero promoted rows**. `python3 -m generalship check` passes, and
the baseline still reports **23/127 eligible engagements in 13 groups**, with strength Brier
**0.276882** against **0.250000** for equal odds.
