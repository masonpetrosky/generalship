# Liberation 1813 first pass v1

Primary-verified campaign pass, 2026-10-19. All **52/52 frozen entries** now have draft dossiers: **696 claims, 302 explicit unknowns and 447 citations**, with all nine dimensions and **104 side-level completion records**. Cohort coverage is **261/325 in 12/16 complete groups**; **64 entries** remain without drafts.

The group includes the spring and autumn battles, Caldiero, repeated-name encounters and blockades ending in 1814. Küstrin’s exact start stays null. No entry or source was selected by outcome, reputation or ease of obtaining a force figure. Frozen frame, cohort, outcomes and model inputs remain unchanged.

## What the pass preserves

- **Force populations.** Different dated or component observations remain alternatives. Lützen’s listed French army is not all engaged; Leipzig inventories include later arrivals. Kulm’s first-day Ostermann remainder differs from the second-day allied force. Terminal fortress garrisons, sick, officials and returned troops do not reconstruct opening strength. Opponent estimates are labelled and unmatched own-side returns remain missing.
- **Arithmetic.** Plotho’s Luckau 13,300 Prussians + 2,500 Russians sums to 15,800 against a printed 15,000 summary. Danzig’s late national components sum to 14,700 against about 15,000; separately stated hospital cases are not automatically added. Bodart’s Danzig page really prints 4,000 land besiegers, separately from fleet crews; it is not silently changed to 40,000.
- **Authority.** Superior, local and contingent roles remain distinct: Langeron/Barclay at Thorn, Alexander/Wittgenstein at Bautzen, crown-prince/Bülow orders, Saint-Cyr/Klenau/Tolstoy at the Dresden blockade, Narbonne/Dutaillis at Torgau, and Davout/Gérard at Hamburg. Incompatible labels, including Stettin and Hüningen, remain disputed. No automatic equal credit or continuous appointment is inferred.
- **Posture and works.** Dresden’s 26 August allied attack and 27 August French attack have separate scoped claims. Katzbach’s competing planned offensives support `both_attacked`; a defender’s local counterattack alone does not. Named walls, château, abatis, palisades and redoubts are retained; dykes are not automatically military works. Commander-created adaptations require an explicit construction attribution, distinct from inherited permanent fabric.
- **Chronology and outcomes.** Küstrin’s 7 March agreement and 30 March handover, Hamburg’s 12 May command change and later evacuation, and Magdeburg’s 14 May agreement and later columns/occupation remain separate. Stettin, Danzig, Belfort and Hüningen dates differ by source/definition. Bodart’s Peterswalde heading prints 1809 against the frozen 1813 crosswalk. Narrow end-state alternatives at Königswartha, Liebertwolkwitz and Wilhelmsburg remain visible without recoding the frozen winner.
