### Independence groups

- **Humphreys** is the author of the registered *From Gettysburg to the Rapidan* (Bristoe, Mine
  Run). The new book is placed in that author's existing group,
  `humphreys-gettysburg-rapidan-1883`, whose name reflects the earlier volume. The two books are
  one author family, not independent witnesses.
- He was the Army of the Potomac's chief of staff. By his preface he used the War Department's
  papers, Meade's retained despatches and his own papers, and Sheridan's dispatches are
  addressed to him. The Humphreys, Meade and Sheridan families therefore share headquarters
  papers and are not independent of one another. Each dependency note records this.
- New author groups: `meade-overland-report`, `sheridan-overland-reports`,
  `hampton-trevilian-report`, `butler-wilsons-wharf-dispatches` and `wild-wilsons-wharf-report`.
  Both OR parts share the container group `or-series-i-volume-xxxvi`.
- Butler was not at Wilson's Wharf. His telegrams relay subordinate reports and are not
  independent of Wild's.

## Decisions and limits

- **Scope and overlaps.** Some frozen records fall inside the intervals of others:
  - Haw's Shop (May 28) and Old Church (May 30) are inside Totopotomoy Creek (May 28–30);
  - Yellow Tavern (May 11) is inside Spotsylvania (May 8–21);
  - Wilson's Wharf (May 24) is inside the North Anna (May 23–26);
  - Trevilian Station (June 11–12) is inside Cold Harbor (May 31–June 12).

  Each dossier names the others. Raid-wide and period totals are not assigned to one record:
  - Sheridan's 350, 379 and 425 for May 9–25;
  - Humphreys' May 28–31 and June 1–12 totals;
  - Hampton's 852 prisoners for June 8–24.

  Actions outside the frozen dates are context only, among them Beaver Dam, Meadow Bridge, the
  May 21 rear guard, Hawes's Shop on June 3, the June 20–21 White House fight and the June 23
  picket fight.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Eight records have blank frozen bounds (VA052, VA055,
  VA056, VA057, VA058, VA059, VA099, VA066). Every figure found is recorded with its date, basis
  and scope, and none is adopted:
  - Humphreys' returns: Army of the Potomac 99,438 on April 30; Ninth Corps 923 officers and
    18,408 men; his reconstruction of not less than 61,953 for Lee's army on May 1; Early's
    13,000 muskets; Ewell's 8,000 and 6,000;
  - reinforcements: 8,000 with Tyler; about 12,000 effective sent from May 4 to June 12; Hoke,
    Breckinridge and Pickett, 8,700 muskets in all, against Badeau's 9,162 for Pickett; Hoke's
    division under 6,000; Smith's 12,500/10,000;
  - North Anna estimates: Hancock's 24,000 and Lee's possible 36,000;
  - cavalry: Sheridan's corps of about 10,000 on May 4; the Haw's Shop brigade "reported to be
    4,000 strong";
  - Wilson's Wharf: Butler's 1,800 garrison, matching the frozen description's 1,800. The basis
    of the CWSAC figure is unestablished and may be the OR telegram itself, so the match is not
    treated as corroboration. Wild's estimate of the attackers is garbled in OCR. The frozen description's 3,000 for Fitzhugh Lee
    is not corroborated in the inspected passages.
- **Disputes preserved** (27 claims marked `disputed`: nine force-scope, six result, ten
  casualty and two information claims):
  - **Prisoners at the salient.** Meade gives "over 3,000"; Hancock, via Humphreys, gives
    "nearly 4,000".
  - **Bethesda Church, May 30.** NPS says the Union was driven back; Humphreys says the attack
    was repulsed.
  - **Haw's Shop.** NPS says the Union advance was stopped; Humphreys says the works were
    carried. The Confederate force was the whole corps (Sheridan) or two divisions and Butler's
    brigade (Fitzhugh Lee, via Humphreys).
  - **End of the Cold Harbor offensive.** Meade gives about 11 a.m.; Humphreys gives half-past
    one. The two may describe different events (the end of the assaults and the suspension
    order), so the conflict may be one of reference rather than fact.
  - **Relative numbers at Trevilian.** Hampton cites "disparity in numbers" in a report covering
    June 8–24; Sheridan says the enemy was reinforced by infantry, and was led to believe the
    enemy's cavalry was superior in numbers over the whole period of his report.
  - **Saint Mary's Church.** Sheridan says the "entire cavalry corps" attacked; Hampton says
    Lomax held the river road. Sheridan has an orderly retirement and Hampton a rout.
  - **Wilson's Wharf losses.** Butler gives 1 killed; Wild's line begins "2 killed".
  - **Wilson's Wharf summons and composition.** Butler says Lee's flag came before the attack;
    Wild says after an hour and a half of fighting. Butler reports cavalry, infantry and
    artillery; Wild supposed cavalry with three guns.
  - **Sheridan's knowledge of Hunter.** His June 16 dispatch says he had no definite information
    and that report placed Hunter at Staunton; his 1866 report says prisoners taken on the 11th
    placed Hunter at or near Lexington.
  - **Frozen against live casualties:**
    - Old Church: 900 against 90;
    - Wilderness: 29,800 against 28,800;
    - Spotsylvania: 30,000 against 27,000;
    - North Anna: 4,000 against 5,140;
    - Totopotomoy: 2,200 against 1,890;
    - Cold Harbor: 15,500 against 18,000;
    - Trevilian: 1,600 against 2,078;
    - Saint Mary's Church: 630 against 0.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - For the army battles the frozen rows list Grant, Meade and Lee. The live pages omit Meade
    and give Lee as Major General; Trevilian and Saint Mary's give Hampton as Lieutenant
    General. The live ranks are not adopted.
  - Quoted directions are attributed as the sources give them: Grant's decisions to move left
    and to assault on June 3; Meade's orders to Warren and his suspension of the Cold Harbor
    attack; Hampton's directions to Fitzhugh Lee.
  - Humphreys' note that Warren, overseeing the withdrawal, was not at the head of his column
    when it was obstructed by the Provost-Marshal-General's mounted troops on the night of
    May 7–8 (Humphreys calls this his most important duty) is attributed as a finding about the
    obstruction, not as criticism of Warren.
  - Criticisms are also attributed: the misdirected order to Smith and Sheridan's objection to
    escorting the train.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** Two terrain claims are tagged `inherited`: the Wilderness forest and the
  Totopotomoy swamps. All works and positions taken during the operations stay `unresolved`.
  Outcomes are `post_outcome`.
- **Selection overlap.** The Humphreys `north-anna` range (ending at character 323,465) and the
  `cavalry-corps-yellow-tavern` range (starting at 322,286) overlap by 1,179 characters, so the
  start of the cavalry chapter appears in both sections. The registry's inspection note does
  not say so; the immutable record is left unchanged.
- **OCR limits.** Page locators rely on OCR running heads. Two were inferred or misread: p.163
  reads "leg" and p.111 reads "Ill". Several quotes preserve OCR corruption, for example
  "10 000", "oyer 3,000" and "Our w loss". Quotes cited from garbled lines are limited to their
  legible parts.

## Review correction

Each required finding was checked against the retained selection text before any change; every
new quote occurs exactly once in its section, and the dossiers were regenerated from the
builder. All twelve hold and are applied:

- **OVR-R1** (VA046 `contact-reports`): a quarter past seven is when Meade *received* Warren's
  despatch, not when Warren sent it. Value corrected; p.23 citation added.
- **OVR-R2** (VA046 `command-roles`): Humphreys does not say Meade had learned of Ewell's
  approach; the attack "would soon develop what part of Lee's army was there". Value corrected;
  citation added.
- **OVR-R3** (VA048 `command-roles` and this memo): Humphreys' p.58 footnote places the
  obstruction of Warren's column at about eleven o'clock on the night march begun on the evening
  of May 7, names the Provost-Marshal-General's mounted troops as the cause and calls Warren's
  staying at the Lacy house "the most important part of his duty". Value, rationale and the
  command-roles bullet above corrected; four citations added (the reviewer's three plus the
  "half-past eight in the evening" start of the march, for the date).
- **OVR-R4** (VA048 `casualty-records`): the selected passage does not say the Cavalry Corps is
  excluded. The rationale now gives Humphreys' May 8–19 scope and says the VA052 raid losses may
  or may not be included, so the records must not be added; citation added.
- **OVR-R5** (VA052 `forage-and-rations`): the half ration for one day was forage, as the 1866
  report says. Value corrected; citation added.
- **OVR-R6** (VA052 `recorded-result`): the Meadow Bridge placement of Gordon's wound was not
  from an inspected passage. Rationale replaced.
- **OVR-R7** (VA056 `reported-force-scope`): "three guns" is Wild's own description, not the
  prisoners'; Butler reports cavalry, infantry and artillery. Value and rationale corrected;
  Butler's composition quote added and Wild's quote extended.
- **OVR-R8** (VA056 `summons-and-reports`): Butler puts the summons before the attack, Wild after
  an hour and a half. Now `disputed`; the flag-timing quote and Wild's "I declined" are cited.
- **OVR-R9** (VA056 `recorded-result`): Butler's repulse statement now has its two citations.
- **OVR-R10** (VA056 `casualty-records`): Butler says the enemy "lost 20" (noun garbled), not
  "about 20 dead". Value corrected; citation added.
- **OVR-R11** (VA099 `hunter-and-rear-reports`): the June 16 dispatch (no definite information;
  Hunter reported at Staunton) and the 1866 report (prisoners of the 11th placed him at or near
  Lexington) are separated. Now `disputed`; two citations added.
- **OVR-R12** (this memo): the validation section was stale. Replaced above.
