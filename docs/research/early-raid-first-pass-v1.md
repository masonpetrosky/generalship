## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** The following fall outside the frozen dates and are context only:
  - the July 7–8 fights at Frederick;
  - the July 16 capture of wagons near Purcellville;
  - the July 19 fight at Ashby's Gap;
  - the July 25 fighting at Martinsburg;
  - Chambersburg (July 30), Hancock (July 31) and Old Town (August 2);
  - New Creek (August 4).

  Campaign-wide levies, captures and loss totals are not assigned to one record.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The frozen imported bounds (Cool Spring 5,000 and 8,000;
  Second Kernstown 10,000 and 13,000) have no source basis in the inspected passages. The other
  records' bounds are blank. Every figure is recorded with its date and scope, and none is
  adopted:
  - Wallace: Tyler's 2,500 and Ricketts's 1,750 and 1,600. He gives the nine veteran
    regiments as 3,350, and estimated the Confederates engaged at about 18,000, later not less
    than 18,000.
  - Pond: Early's army at the start of the march, about 17,000.
  - Early: infantry not over 10,000 before Washington.
  - Barnard, via Pond: about 20,400 available at Washington, 9,000 in the garrison.
  - Pond: Wright's 10,000 effective and 650 on July 13.
  - Averell: 1,350 infantry, 1,000 cavalry and 300 more, against about 5,000; Pond gives
    2,350 and 2,700.
  - Crook: 3,000 or 4,000 men broke at Kernstown.
  - Kelley: estimates of 2,500 to 4,500 for the raiders.
  - Johnson: two regiments with under 130 men in camp.

  The frozen "Divisions(5,850 total)" for Rutherford's Farm has no inspected basis.
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - Monocacy terrain: Wallace says the fords are few; Pond says the river was fordable
    everywhere.
  - Monocacy losses: frozen 2,359, live 2,094, Wallace's table 1,968 (OCR) and Pond's 1,959.
    Wallace's telegram says not over 200 prisoners. The table's footnote says many counted as
    missing probably returned to duty, and Pond puts the total between about 1,500 and over 2,000.
  - Fort Stevens, July 12: Early says his skirmishers repulsed three attacks; Pond says they
    were driven back a mile.
  - Fort Stevens losses: frozen 874 and live 873, against Pond's 280 engaged and 400–500 in
    total.
  - Cool Spring: the Confederate 397 has no inspected source. Crook and Pond agree on 422.
  - Rutherford's Farm losses: frozen 1,100 and live 742. Averell gives 214 Union and 203 killed
    and wounded plus 267 captured Confederates; Pond says the Confederate loss was probably over
    400.
  - Second Kernstown: Crook says his left was thrown into some confusion, then reformed, and his
    line fell back in good order and nothing fell to the enemy.
    Pond says the line broke and the enemy counted 12 caissons and 72 wagons.
  - Folck's Mill losses: frozen 38 and live 60, against Kelley's 8 dead and 30-odd wounded and
    Pond's 30.
  - Moorefield: Pond gives 420 prisoners and a Union loss of 41. Johnson reports about 300 missing
    on reaching the Valley, of whom 150 had come in; his net loss reads as about 150, but the
    sentence is ambiguous.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live pages give Early as Major General; the frozen rank is Lieutenant General.
  - Crook (frozen Brigadier General) and Kelley sign as brevet major generals.
  - The live pages list only Wright and Early at Fort Stevens, not McCook.
  - Wallace's and Crook's reports describe their own orders. At the Monocacy bridge Wallace's
    August report has Tyler take command and obey the order to hold it; his July 10 telegram
    credits Colonel Brown.
  - Johnson's charges against McCausland are recorded as an interested subordinate's
    allegations, not an adjudication.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit, and live ranks are not adopted.
- **Tags.** Only Fort Stevens' fortifications are tagged `inherited`: both accounts say the
  works existed when Early arrived. All other claims stay `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Campaign contribution.** Delay to Early's march is recorded as campaign contribution, not
  tactical outcome. So are the "Battle that Saved Washington" label and the Moorefield
  cavalry's later weakness.
- **Families.**
  - Crook's family covers two records.
  - Johnson's single report is split across two volumes; only the Volume XLIII portion is
    selected.
  - Early's July 14 report and his Volume XLIII reports for Sheridan's Valley Campaign share
    one group, `early-valley-1864-reports`.
  - Authors with earlier registry groups (Early, Averell, Wallace) are noted in the dependency
    notes and placed in new 1864 groups. The primary may prefer to reuse the existing groups.

The seven null unknowns are the opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability, causal
effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The cohort, both admission proposals and the
baseline are unchanged, with **zero promoted rows**. The baseline still uses **23/127 engagements in
13 eligible groups**, with strength Brier **0.276882** against **0.250000** for equal odds.

## Review correction

A separate Claude Opus 5.5 `high` review of this pass and Sheridan's Valley Campaign
(`valley-review-586811f-opus-high-v1`, reviewing `586811f`) required thirteen corrections and made
ten advisories. Each finding was checked against the retained selection text before any change;
every new quote occurs in its cited section, and the dossiers were regenerated from the builder.
The findings for this campaign all hold and are applied:

- **VALLEY-R01** (MD007 `casualty-records`): the Wallace table's footnote on the missing ("Many
  of the men here counted as missing probably returned to duty") and Pond's stragglers remark and
  1,500 to over-2,000 range (relaying Wallace's suggestion that Tyler's reports were imperfect)
  are added, with four citations. The rationale says they qualify the totals and are not separate
  counts.
- **VALLEY-R02** (VA116 `casualty-records`): Hayes's 396 is one of Duval's two brigades and so a
  component of Duval's 513, not an addition. Pond's "somewhat uncertain" qualification and
  Colonel Campbell as the source of the 317 are added; the rationale forbids summing the
  components. Four citations added.
- **VALLEY-R03** (WV013 `casualty-records` and this memo): Johnson reached the Valley with about
  300 missing, 150 of whom had come in, "leaving that number as my net loss". The value now reads
  this as about 150 and states that the antecedent is ambiguous. The Moorefield dispute bullet
  above is corrected.
- **VALLEY-R10** (VA115 `scouts-and-underestimate`): the Ramseur sentence is Pond's own footnote
  text after the Rodes quotation ends. Value and rationale corrected.
- **VALLEY-R13** (this memo): the counts were stale. At `586811f` the repository held 153 draft
  dossiers, and this pass took the registry from 643/622 to 670/649. Corrected above.

The Sheridan's Valley findings (R04–R09, R11, R12) are recorded in [its
