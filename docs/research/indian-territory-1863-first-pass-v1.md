## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** The train's march from Baxter Springs, the Greenleaf Prairie fight, Blunt's crossing
  of the Arkansas (July 15–16), Cabell's arrival after Honey Springs, the Perryville raid, the
  August 31 skirmishes and the occupation of Fort Smith are context.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Figures recorded and not adopted:
  - Cabin Creek: the frozen CS 1,600 (both bounds, although its text says 1,600–1,800); the live
    1,500; Williams's 1,600–1,800 from prisoners and his attacking column of less than 900;
    Britton's sixteen hundred for Watie, fifteen hundred for Cabell (who could not cross) and
    Foreman's six hundred.
  - Honey Springs: the live 3,000 for Blunt; Britton's unit and gun counts (eight regiments and
    two squadrons against three regiments and three battalions; twelve Union guns against four);
    Cooper's aide's estimate of 4,000 Federals, which Cooper thought 500 low; Cabell's upwards of
    two thousand arriving after the battle.
  - Devil's Backbone: Cabell's over 3,000 aggregate, never more than 1,600 in the field or 1,200
    for duty, about 1,250 on August 31 and no more than 900 afterwards; his estimates of 2,300
    Federals and of Cloud's 1,500 cavalry, six guns and 40 wagons of infantry.
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - Cabin Creek losses: frozen 88 (US 23; CS 65) and live 80 (US 21; CS 59); Williams's 1 killed
    and about 20 wounded against Britton's official three killed and thirty wounded; Williams's
    estimate of 50 Confederates killed, as many wounded and 9 prisoners.
  - Honey Springs losses: frozen 716 (CS 637) against live 211 (CS 134); Blunt's claim, relayed
    by Britton, of 150 Confederate dead buried, 400 wounded and 77 prisoners against Cooper's 134
    killed and wounded and 47 prisoners; Blunt's 17 and 60 against Cooper's claim that the
    Federal loss exceeded 200.
  - Devil's Backbone result: Cabell's repulse of the enemy and orderly withdrawal against
    Britton's and NPS's account of regiments breaking and retiring in disorder.
  - Devil's Backbone losses: frozen 81 (US 16; CS 65) and live 31 (US 14; CS 17); Cabell's 5
    killed and 12 wounded (6 and 14 over two days) and his claim of about 30 Federal killed and
    100–150 wounded against Cloud's two killed and twelve wounded (relayed by Britton). The 80
    provost prisoners carried off are not battle casualties.
  - Britton names Cloud's regiment the Sixth Missouri State Militia Cavalry; the frozen text reads
    6th Missouri Cavalry.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live Honey Springs page styles Blunt a Brigadier General (frozen Major General). Live ranks
    are not adopted.
  - At Cabin Creek, Williams laid the plan as ranking officer while Dodd commanded the escort. At
    Honey Springs, Cooper says Tandy Walker mistook an order and took the reserve away. At Devil's
    Backbone, Blunt detached Cloud; Cabell says several regiments ran.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** The concealed Cabin Creek works and Cooper's July 14 dispositions predate the
  actions, but their timing and effect are not established, so no claim is tagged `inherited`.
  All claims stay `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.

The three null unknowns are the opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability,
causal effect or new commander ranking is introduced. No model input, cohort file, admission
proposal or baseline is changed.
