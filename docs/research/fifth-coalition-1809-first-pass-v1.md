## Findings and limits

- **Populations and times:** Bodart’s available-for-engagement totals remain distinct from local
  detachments, garrisons, arrivals and engaged subsets. Raszyn separates 30,000 men from 5,000
  horses in Welden’s earlier assembly; these are not 35,000 people. Pordenone separates Sahuc’s
  advance guard from whole armies. Hausen’s Austrian marching column excludes Extrakorps;
  Schierling and Eckmühl do not reuse the same troops as two independent campaign contributions.
  Regensburg’s five defending city battalions are distinct from the retreating field army.
  Aspern retains Bodart’s day-one/day-two engaged subsets separately from available totals.
  Graz separates Gambin’s roughly 1,300 from Broussier’s division and the Ban’s larger force.
- **Printed errors and alternatives:** Präwald’s `ca. 10.0000` is preserved as an anomalous printed
  French numeral, not repaired into 10,000. Welden’s Wagram table distinguishes all-ranks
  marching strength from firearms/fighting strength, then deducts detached troops. Its printed
  105,635 infantry plus 12,669 cavalry do not equal its 118,322 total: eighteen men remain an
  explicit arithmetic discrepancy. National force comparisons are not averaged or judged by reputation.
- **Disputes and clocks:** the candidate Ostrowek/Góra bridgehead action is dated 3 May in Welden,
  against frozen Ostrowiec 26 April; similar commander names do not settle the crosswalk.
  May Sandomir retains Welden’s 800 town garrison versus Sołtyk/Bodart’s 4,000; June’s different
  garrison has its own 4,000/estimated 5,000 alternative. Pelet dates delivery of Präwald’s works
  to 21 May versus Bodart’s 20th. Raab’s siege retains 1,500/2,500 garrison figures and conditional
  delivery on the 24th versus Bodart’s 22-June endpoint. Pelet’s compressed August Innsbruck
  sequence differs from the frozen 13th. Aspern’s outcome is disputed by Pelet, who calls it at
  most indecisive; Bodart’s Austrian winner remains frozen. Hargrove’s 3,500 marching out of
  Flushing and Pelet’s 4,000 surrendering are distinct from island forces and opening garrison.
- **Authority:** Poniatowski/Ferdinand army direction is separated from Sokolnicki, Mohr, Mondet,
  Geringer and local town commanders. Tyrol separates regular command from insurgent leaders.
  Italy distinguishes Eugène/Johann direction from Sahuc, Frimont, Grenier, Seras, Giulay,
  Broussier, Gambin, Péchy and other local tasks. Bavaria records Napoleon’s orders alongside
  Lannes/Davout/Lefebvre/Bessières and local Austrian authority. Znaim distinguishes Marmont’s
  initial attack and Masséna’s arrival from later armistice authority; Schwarzenberg’s recent
  assumption of cavalry-reserve command stays visible. No automatic attribution to all names.
- **Missingness and dependence:** matched dated own-side local returns remain missing for many
  entries. The assigned Kriegsarchiv volume I ends on 23 April, and its later Neumarkt–Ebelsberg
  volume was not located in bounded catalogue navigation. Those Austrian gaps stay explicit.
  Oberau and August Tyrol have particularly thin inspected reporting. The inspected national,
  participant and staff histories transmit documents and opponent estimates; their originals
  were not separately inspected. A source-backed claim can still be historically wrong.
