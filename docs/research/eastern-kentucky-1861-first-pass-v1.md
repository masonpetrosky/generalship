## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** Zollicoffer's advances to Cumberland Ford and the Rockcastle River, Sill's march by
  John's Creek and the Piketon skirmish, Williams's retreat toward Pound Gap, and McCook's advance to
  Munfordville are context only.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The frozen imported bounds are Barbourville US 300 and CS
  800, Camp Wildcat US 7,000, Ivy Mountain CS 1,010 and Rowlett's Station CS 1,350; the other sides
  are blank. The CWSAC Barbourville figures match Zollicoffer's report, and the Rowlett's Station
  1,350 equals Hindman's 1,100 infantry and 250 cavalry; Ivy Mountain's 1,010 is Williams's whole
  force including sick and extra-duty men, not the men engaged. These matches are not
  corroboration. Every figure is recorded with its date and scope, and none is adopted:
  - Camp Wildcat: each side attributes about 7,000 to the other (Schoepf's prisoners; Zollicoffer's
    "said to be 7,000", later nine regiments). NPS's Union 7,000 is not traced to a return.
  - Ivy Mountain: Nelson's 700 in ambush; Williams's 300 against 1,500–2,000 (November 9), with his
    command "now" (after the fight) nine infantry and five mounted companies, 1,100 in all; and his
    250 at the pass, 1,600 and 1,800 in the two columns and 1,010 in all (November 13). The CWSAC's
    two mounted companies do not match his five.
  - Rowlett's Station: Willich's 375 men and 43 officers and sergeants against a force at least
    seven times as strong; Hindman's "more than double" his own.
- **Disputes preserved** (10 claims marked `disputed`: three force-scope, three result, three
  casualty and one logistics claim):
  - **Camp Wildcat.** Schoepf says the enemy retreated; Zollicoffer describes a reconnaissance in
    force after which he fell back. Casualties: frozen 78 against live 96; Schoepf's 4 killed and 18
    wounded against Zollicoffer's 42 wounded and 11 killed and missing (the CWSAC CS 53).
  - **Ivy Mountain.** Nelson's pursuit against Williams's "The enemy did not pursue". Nelson dates the
    fight 1 p.m. on the 8th; Williams says "yesterday" on November 9 and "1.30 o'clock on the 9th" on
    November 13. Nelson says the rebels fled after an hour and twenty minutes; Williams's November 9
    report says the engagement lasted about four hours. Nelson's own killed figure reads "0" in OCR
    although he says the first discharge killed 4; Williams's losses rise from 10 killed and 7 or 8
    wounded to 10, 15 and 40 missing, and his estimate of the Union dead from 150 to over 300.
  - **Rowlett's Station.** Willich has the enemy disappearing in disorder; Hindman withdrew because
    the enemy had crossed in force; NPS cites McCook's approach. Willich's 38 against the frozen 40,
    Hindman's 14 against the frozen 91. Willich says the pontoon bridge was finished on the evening
    of the 15th; NPS says it was completed on December 17.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - By their own reports, Willich was at division headquarters when the general alarm was given
    (Von Trebra ordered the companies forward), and Hindman, whose report narrates the first exchange
    of skirmish fire, went to choose a camp during a lull of about half an hour, leaving Terry in
    command; the firing recommenced before he returned.
  - At Ivy Mountain Williams went forward but returned to camp at daylight; Captain May's party held
    the pass. Nelson's colonels Harris and Norton led the ascent.
  - Only NPS names Captain Black as the Barbourville home guard commander.
  - The live page gives Nelson as Major General; it is not adopted. No listed commander receives
    automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** The Ivy Mountain defile is tagged `inherited`. All other claims stay `unresolved` or
  `post_outcome`.

The five null unknowns are the four opening strengths and the Barbourville terrain, for which no
inspected passage describes the ground. No morale/readiness score, probability, causal effect or
commander ranking is introduced. No model input, frozen baseline or admission proposal is changed.

## Review correction

Each finding for this campaign was checked against the retained selection text (or the retained NPS
text) before any change; every new quote occurs once in its section, and the dossiers were regenerated
from the builder.

KY003 and KY004 are revised under `eastern-kentucky-1861-review-correction-2026-09-25` and supersede
byte-for-byte archives at `data/evidence/history/KY003.v1.json` and `KY004.v1.json`. KY001 and KY002
are unchanged.

- **W61-R8** (KY003 `reported-force-scope`): Williams's November 9 figure of 1,100 is his command "now",
  after the fight, and his November 13 report gives no company count. The value and rationale now say
  so: the difference is between the CWSAC's two mounted companies and his five, and his two reports
  differ on the men at the pass (about 300 against 250). One p.228 citation ("and two of these not
  fall") added.
- **W61-R9** (KY004 `command-roles` and this memo): Hindman narrates the first skirmish exchange, left
  to choose a camp during a half-hour lull, and the firing recommenced before he returned. Value and
  rationale corrected; the single citation is replaced by two p.20 citations (the first extended to
  "leaving Colonel Terry in command", which the value still states).

Advisories:

- **Adopted:** A4 (KY004 `pontoon-bridge`: NPS's December 17 completion against Willich's evening of
  the 15th; citation added and the claim is now `disputed`), A11 (KY003 `recorded-result`: Williams's
  "lasting about four hours" against Nelson's hour and twenty minutes; citation added).
- **Not adopted:** A12 (the OCR heading of Zollicoffer's October 20 report also reads "1801"). The
  section date 1861-10-20 is correct; noting the OCR year in the inspection field would need a
  metadata-only successor for a record whose content and dates are right, so it is recorded here only.

The review correction adds no source records. Citations rise from 148 to 152 and disputed claims from 9
