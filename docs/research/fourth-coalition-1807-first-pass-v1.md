  duty strength and provisioning populations. Danzig separates initial investment from
  progressive employment and end strength. Neisse and Glatz separate whole garrisons,
  camp assignments, mounted horses, pickets and assault subsets. Heilsberg retains Bodart's
  65,000/95,000 available and 49,000/53,000 engaged alongside Höpfner's joint estimate.
  Friedland keeps morning fighting strength separate from troops later directed there.
  Buenos Aires distinguishes landed effectives from before-city rank and file and detached troops.
  Stralsund's treaty establishment is not an observed Swedish garrison count.
- **Authority:** superior, local, technical and civic roles remain distinct. Schweidnitz's
  senior Kropf handled supply without displacing the commandants. Savary acted for sick
  Lannes at Ostrolenka. Heilsberg's temporary Gortschakow assumption has no secure clock.
  Kosel's illness-related delegations are not reduced to one permanent replacement event.
  Kolberg's royal instruction and force/arms changes do not identify a commander effect.
- **Disputes:** French Mohrungen bulletins differ on Dupont's participation and the Russian
  command label. Eylau, Lomitten, Guttstadt/Deppen and Heilsberg preserve conflicting result
  accounts. Buenos Aires testimony dates the main assault to 5 July against Bodart's 6 July
  entry; its defended-buildings account and Bodart's named fortress works remain source-specific.
  Capitulation, march-out, intended conditional handover and actual possession are distinct.
  No frozen winner, date, cohort or frame is changed.
- **Source limits:** Höpfner is Prussian war-archive history with mediated Russian and French
  information. Napoleon's letters and public bulletins share one interested imperial family.
  The Friedland order is printed through Thibaudeau/Dumas, not an inspected autograph.
  Groussac's Argentine retrospective work uses British trial material; Key-Åberg's Swedish
  diplomatic history mediates military accounts. Neither substitutes for the missing original
  Spanish or Swedish dated military return. The British trial is a contemporary commercial
