## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.**
  - Context only: the May 13-14 skirmishes before New Market, Hunter's May 26-June 4 advance, the June
    8 junction with Crook and Averell, Duffie's raid, the Lexington delay, the pursuit through Liberty
    and the loss of guns near Salem.
  - Piedmont's frozen interval includes the occupation of Staunton on June 6.
  - Lynchburg's includes the June 17 fight at Diamond Hill, which Pond calls Quaker church.
  - Campaign-wide losses, including Strother's claims, are not assigned to one record.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Every figure is recorded with its date, basis and scope, and
  none is adopted.
  - New Market:
    - frozen and live 6,275 US and 4,090 CS; the NPS description gives Sigel 10,000 and the
      Confederates about 4,100;
    - Sigel: 5,500 against 8,000-9,000, then six regiments besides artillery and cavalry against
      about 7,000 infantry;
    - Pond: Sigel's field force at most six or seven thousand; about 4,000 infantry, 1,000 cavalry
      and three batteries on May 1; Breckinridge nearly 5,000;
    - the Confederate report of the day after the battle, cited by Pond: 4,282 infantry and
      artillery present;
    - Imboden, cited by Pond: 800 cavalry, and 3,440 infantry and 350 artillery engaged;
    - Pond's reconstruction: 4,600 to 5,000;
    - Echols's brigade, from an inspection abstract: 2,150 on May 6.
  - Piedmont:
    - frozen and live 8,500 US and 5,500 CS;
    - Pond: Hunter left Cedar Creek on May 26 with 8,500 and twenty-one guns;
    - Hunter: 6,000-7,000 and 16 guns, from papers on Jones's body;
    - Vaughn: an aggregate of 5,600 in the fight and not over 3,000 effective the next day (Pond
      quotes "effectives"); Hunter at 9,000-10,000 infantry, then about 10,000.
  - Lynchburg:
    - frozen 44,000 (battle field), with the side rows blank and live zeros;
    - Hunter and Pond: Crook and Averell brought about 10,000 on June 8; Pond puts Hunter at about
      18,000 with thirty guns before detachments;
    - Hunter's reports of the defenders, all rumors, estimates or prisoners' statements: a few
      thousand invalids and militia, 10,000-15,000, Ewell's (Early's) corps of 20,000, and at least
      double his strength;
    - Pond: half of Early's corps arrived on the 17th.
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - New Market:
    - objective: Lynchburg's railroad and canal (NPS) against Sigel's stated aim to threaten Staunton
      and assist Crook (quoted by Pond);
    - result: Breckinridge says the enemy fled; Sigel says the withdrawal was gradual and in perfect
      order;
    - losses:
      - frozen 1,380 (840; 540) against live 1,361 (841; 520);
      - Sigel's about 600 killed and wounded and 50 prisoners;
      - Pond's 831 and 531;
      - five guns (Sigel) or six (other accounts, per Pond).
  - Piedmont losses:
    - frozen 2,375 (875; 1,500) against live 2,475 (875; 1,600);
    - Hunter: over 1,000 prisoners, 600 Confederate killed and wounded estimated, and his own loss of
      under 500 through June 8;
    - Pond: about 1,500 prisoners including the Staunton sick and wounded, and a Union loss of 420;
    - Vaughn: 1,500 Union killed and wounded, 1,000 prisoners and 60 of his wounded left.
  - Lynchburg:
    - who attacked on the 18th: Hunter says the enemy sallied and was routed; Early says the enemy
      assaulted and was repulsed; Pond says Hunter attacked in force and Early's infantry sallied and
      was driven back;
    - who directed the defense: Hunter credits the repulse to Breckinridge, and Early claims it for
      his command;
    - losses: Pond's about two hundred Union, Hunter's "comparatively light", frozen 900 and live 900
      (700; 200).
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live page lists Sigel as Colonel and Early as Major General; the live ranks are not adopted.
  - Stahel and Imboden (New Market), Thoburn (the decisive flank attack at Piedmont, on Hunter's
    order), Vaughn (after Jones's death) and Breckinridge (Lynchburg's defense before Early) are not
    listed commanders.
  - No listed commander receives automatic credit.
- **Tags.** No ground is tagged `inherited`. The Piedmont works were Confederate field works of
  unstated age, and the Lynchburg works were being extended during the action. Claims stay
  `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Campaign contribution.** Hunter's retreat through West Virginia, which opened the Valley to
  Early's advance (NPS), Lee's detachment of Early's corps, and the destruction at Staunton and
  Lexington are campaign context, not tactical outcome.
- **Families.**
  - Hunter is a new author group, `hunter-lynchburg-1864-reports`; Vaughn is new,
    `vaughn-piedmont-1864-reports`.
  - Sigel's 1864 dispatches are placed in his only registered group, `sigel-carthage-report` (1861).
  - Breckinridge's dispatch is placed in his only registered group,
    `breckinridge-baton-rouge-1862-report`.
  - Early's dispatch is in `early-valley-1864-reports`, and Pond stays in
    `pond-shenandoah-valley-1883`.
  - Same-author groups are not independent corroboration.

The three null unknowns are the opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability, causal
effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The cohort, admission proposals and baseline are
unchanged, with **zero promoted rows**.
