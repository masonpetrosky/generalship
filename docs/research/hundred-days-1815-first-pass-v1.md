## Material alternatives preserved

- **Tolentino:** Bodart gives Neapolitan/Austrian 29,000/11,000. Colletta gives 12,000/at-least-16,000 on May 3, apart from a wider 24,000 Neapolitan army and an intended 16,000 allocation. No force ratio is chosen. Colletta's Ancona surrender is the day after May 28 (May 29), against the frozen May 30; Gaeta's narrative continuation does not independently date its endpoints.
- **Belgian field populations:** Gilly's Bodart figures are engaged troops, not all available armies. At Quatre-Bras the narratives separate the initial Dutch-Belgian position, successive reinforcements and French troops which did not fight there. Vaudoncourt's French 16,100 infantry/4,910 cavalry/56 guns differs from Siborne's later 15,750/5,165/50 and from Bodart; artillery people are not supplied. At Ligny, Vaudoncourt's infantry/cavalry table, including its inconsistent Prussian corps labels, is not made into Bodart's all-arms total. Plotho and Vaudoncourt give 288 Prussian guns, against Bodart's 224.
- **Waterloo:** Bodart's combined 120,000 opponents includes 67,700 Anglo-Dutch-German and 52,300 Prussians. This is not a simultaneous opening population. Siborne's narrative gives Anglo-allied 67,655; its appendix gives 67,661, with a six-person KGL cavalry difference. Its French total 71,947 depends in part on prior-loss deductions attributed to Napoleon's memoirs. Its time-staged Prussian appendix gives 51,944, with later arrivals separately described; Vaudoncourt's pre-battle Anglo-Batavian estimate is about 80,000. Sources and stages remain separate.
- **Wavre/Namur:** Plotho estimates Grouchy's pursuing force at 50,000 and the Prussian remainder after a brigade leaves at 16,000, against Bodart's 32,000/24,000. Vaudoncourt's changing infantry and *chevaux* components are retained without silently converting horses into people. At Namur neither the compilation nor the bounded narratives establishes complete local personnel counts. Unit components, retreating columns and Teste's final town defense remain distinct. A Prussian occupation and an accomplished French covering withdrawal need not receive the same tactical/campaign label.
- **Eastern operations:** Belfort keeps the whole interval. Primary verification corrected the Chavannes component's timing: Plotho places it June 28, before the frozen June 29 opening, while Vaudoncourt dates Chavannes June 29. The July 8 capture of Pérouse/Bavilliers is a local episode, not a replacement for the whole-interval frozen winner. At Hausbergen the narratives give Hohenzollern/Mazzuchelli/Baden local roles against Bodart's Crown Prince label, and disagree on ordered French retreat versus flight. Military works, rivers, commander-created abatis/inundations and natural relief are not interchangeable controls.
- **Fortress milestones:** Maubeuge's frozen June 21–July 12 differs from Plotho's June 20 investment, July 11 capitulation and July 14 departure/occupation; Vaudoncourt lists July 14. Landrecies retains July 20 versus July 21 capitulation request and July 23 departure, and Plaige versus terminal commandant Foras. Huningue retains different total/usable/terminal garrisons: Bodart 3,000/1,900, Plotho about 4,000/1,800, and Vaudoncourt's 100+30+5 usable component (135 by arithmetic), followed by his printed 130 and terminal fifty. Its nearly-24,000 opponent estimate is not reconciled with about 15,000. June 25/26, August 14/17 trench openings and August 26/28 closure milestones remain explicit.
