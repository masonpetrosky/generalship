### Independence groups

- **Cox** (`cox-atlanta-1882`, as corrected by NASH-R2) commanded a division and, at Franklin,
  both divisions of the Twenty-third Corps under Schofield. His book is an interested participant
  history for Columbia, Spring Hill, Franklin and Nashville. It draws on War Records Office papers and on
  Hood's, Bate's, Beauregard's and Schofield's reports. It is therefore not independent of the
  Official Records families. His earlier *Atlanta* volume is registered by the Atlanta pass in
  `cox-atlanta-1882`. Following the Humphreys precedent, this book is placed in that author's
  existing group, whose name reflects the earlier volume. The two books are one author family,
  not independent witnesses. (The first pass created `cox-march-to-the-sea-1882`; the
  metadata-only successors listed under "Review correction" carry the corrected group.)
- **Hood's** February 15, 1865 report is one document across volumes; its Tennessee portion and
  the other Volume XLV reports join the Atlanta pass's `hood-atlanta-reports` (NASH-R1).
- **French** already had a registered author group, `french-suffolk-reports`, from his 1863 Suffolk
  reports. Following the Humphreys precedent, the Allatoona reports are placed in that group,
  whose name reflects the earlier reports.
- **Doolittle** was Granger's subordinate and wrote to Granger's headquarters. The
  `doolittle-decatur-report` and `rs-granger-decatur-report` families are separate authors, not
  independent witnesses.
- **Sinclair** (`sinclair-johnsonville-report`) was an inspector who was not present. He relies on
  the garrison's own statements.
- **Forrest's** two volumes form one group, `forrest-1864-reports`. Jordan and Pryor's registered
  *Campaigns of Forrest* (`jordan-pryor-forrest-1868`) was prepared with Forrest and is not
  independent of it.
- **Rousseau** has an 1862 record in the `or-buell` group. This pass creates
  `rousseau-murfreesborough-reports` and says so in the dependency note.
- **Other new groups:** `corse-allatoona-reports`, `or-series-i-volume-xxxix` and
  `or-series-i-volume-xlv`.
- **Hood's addenda** were compiled by his adjutant A. P. Mason and are kept in Hood's family.

## Decisions and limits

- **Scope and overlaps.** Spring Hill (November 29) falls inside Columbia (November 24–29).
  Campaign-wide figures are context and are not assigned to one record:
  - Hood's and Thomas's returns (Cox pp.11–19; Hood's addenda);
  - Hood's 7,547 losses "since the opening of this campaign";
  - Hood's campaign loss ceiling of 10,000;
  - Forrest's expedition-wide losses and property figures.

  Actions outside the frozen dates are context only:
  - the Fort Heiman and Paris Landing captures (October 29–30);
  - Bate's December 4 attack at Overall's Creek;
  - the pursuit after December 16.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Four records have blank frozen bounds (TN034, TN035,
  TN036 and TN038); TN032 and AL004 have a Union bound only. Every figure is recorded with its
  date, basis and scope, and none is adopted:
  - **Allatoona.** Corse gives about 1,500 muskets (October 7) and a 1,944 aggregate (890
    garrison and 1,054 of his own). French had "a little over 2,000" in the assault.
  - **Decatur.** Granger gives 1,500 effective men, then not over 1,800, then 3,000. Doolittle
    fought the first day with fewer than 500 men and recapitulates the garrison as 1,500, 2,500
    and 5,000 on successive days. The defenders estimated the Confederate army at 35,000 and
    40,000.
  - **Johnsonville.** Sinclair's partial list (700 infantry, 800 armed employees, 20 cavalry,
    a battery, a colored artillery company, a section of the quartermaster's battery, two
    captured Parrotts and three gunboats). Thompson estimated 13,000 Confederates.
  - **Army returns.** Cox gives Hood 44,729 on November 6 and 53,938 with Forrest, against
    Beauregard's 35,000 effective. Hood's addenda give 30,600 effective on November 6 and 23,053
    on December 10; whether these include Forrest's cavalry is not established (NASH-A8). Thomas had 54,566 present for duty on October 31.
  - **Nashville.** Cox's components: Smith's nearly 12,000, Steedman's 5,200, about 8,000 at
    Murfreesboro and 10,884 cavalry present for duty. His reconstruction gives Hood about 44,000.
  - **Murfreesboro.** Rousseau's two dispatches say Forrest brought 2,500 cavalry with two
    brigades of infantry (December 8) or with two divisions (December 12).
- **Disputes preserved** (20 claims marked `disputed`: six force-scope, seven casualty, two
  result and five others):
  - **River reinforcement at Decatur.** Granger's 200 men against Doolittle's "about 1,200" on
    the gunboat Stone River.
  - **Who burned the Johnsonville shipping.** Forrest says his batteries did it. Sinclair reports
    as hearsay ("It is said") that King ordered the gunboats burned, and says Thompson directed
    the burning of the transports.
  - **Columbia's evacuation date.** Hood and Cox give the night of the 27th; NPS says the 28th.
  - **Responsibility at Spring Hill.** Hood blames Cheatham. Cox says Hood shifted the blame to a
    subordinate.
  - **Franklin's works.** NPS says 1863 works; Cox says built that morning; Hood says hastily built.
  - **Hood's artillery at Franklin.** Hood says he withheld it; Cox says he is in error.
  - **Murfreesboro result.** Rousseau reports a complete rout; Forrest says the advance was checked.
  - **Frozen against live figures:**
    - Allatoona casualties: 1,505 against 1,603;
    - Decatur casualties: 605 against 355, with both Union reports giving 113;
    - Franklin casualties: 8,587 against 9,933;
    - Nashville casualties: 6,602 against 9,561;
    - Murfreesboro casualties: US 225 and CS 197 against US 208 and CS 214;
    - Spring Hill casualties: Unknown against 850.
  - **French's own Allatoona total.** His text reads 709 in OCR, but his table components sum to
    798 plus one staff officer. The figures are preserved as printed.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live pages give French as Brigadier General and list "Henry Thompson" at Johnsonville.
    The frozen row reads C.R. Thompson, and Sinclair prints "C. E. Thompson". Rousseau styles
    Milroy Major-General. None of these is adopted.
  - Quoted directions are attributed as the sources give them:
    - Corse's dispositions and Tourtellotte's defence of the east hill;
    - French's decision to withdraw;
    - Granger's arrival "just as the fight was closing";
    - King's reported and Thompson's stated burning orders;
    - Schofield's crossings;
    - Wagner's order to fight (from two unnamed officers, via Cox);
    - Thomas leaving assaults to his corps commanders.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** Six terrain claims are tagged `inherited`:
  - the Allatoona cut and redoubts;
  - the Decatur plain;
  - the Johnsonville river and wooded bank;
  - the Duck River bend;
  - the Franklin plain with Fort Granger;
  - Nashville's hills and 1862–63 fortifications.

  All works and positions made during the operations stay `unresolved`. Outcomes are
  `post_outcome`.
- **OCR limits.** Page locators rely on OCR running heads. Misread heads are flagged in the
  locators:
  - Granger pp.695–699;
  - Cox pp.103 and 111;
  - Hood pp.661 and 662.

  Several quotes preserve OCR corruption, for example "Gur loss", "0,000" and "74G".

The eight null unknowns are the eight opening strengths.

No morale/readiness score, probability, causal effect or commander ranking is introduced. The v1
cohort, the frozen baseline and both admission proposals are unchanged, with zero promoted rows.
