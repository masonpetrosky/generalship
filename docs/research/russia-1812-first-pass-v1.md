## Populations, authority and chronology

- **Available versus engaged.** Bodart explicitly limits Kobrin's Russian engaged force to 12,000 from 33,000 available, Gorodeczna's allied engaged force to 24,000 from 40,000, and Smolensk's engaged forces to 45,000 French against 30,000 Russians from much larger armies. These footnotes remain in the claims. Hauthal's 2,401 still under arms in Kobrin, his barely 16,000 deployed Saxon/Austrian force at Gorodeczna, and Boutourlin's 32,000 Russian offensive army refer to different populations. They are neither added nor silently substituted.
- **Dated components.** Droysen gives 14,100 Prussian corps combatants on 26 September; Boutourlin gives 10,000 under arms in Steingell's arriving Finland corps rather than the intended 14,000. Those are components during the Mesoten operation/Riga blockade. Riga's initial garrison, later reinforcement, Prussian corps and Macdonald's multinational corps are different populations. Boutourlin's Valutina figures distinguish initial 2,400 infantry from cumulative/end-phase 15,200 after reinforcements; no losses are subtracted to invent an opening force.
- **Whole armies versus detachments.** Boutourlin's Borodino army is 132,000 including militia and Cossacks, against Bodart's 122,000; French matched own-side strength remains unknown in the inspected plan pages. His Malojaroslavets army of about 80,000 excludes Cossacks and is gathering nearby, not the 24,000 local engaged in Bodart. Chambray's Vyazma 37,500 explicitly excludes stragglers. His earlier Smolensk-arrival table of 37,000 infantry and 5,100 cavalry excludes artillery and is not a simultaneous Krasnoi opening count. Formation, gun and horse numbers are not turned into people.
- **Beresina discrepancy.** Chambray's 26-November-morning narrative says 29,700 combatants; the table caption says 28,700, while its printed columns and individual rows sum to 25,700 infantry + 4,000 cavalry = 29,700. The internal 1,000 difference stays explicit. His isolated soldiers are a separate population. Boutourlin's 14,000 at Studenki is one engaged bank/sector, not the whole Russian army or Bodart's 87,000 available. Bodart's exclusions of French stragglers and the 39,000 Russians left at Kobis are retained.
- **Local and superior command.** The records distinguish Raevsky/Bagration, Ostermann/Konovnitsyn/Pahlen, Kulnev/Wittgenstein, Dauvray's delegated execution, Oudinot's wounding and Saint-Cyr's handover, Barclay/Korf and the two Tuchkov ranks, Eugene/Delzons/Dokhturov versus Napoleon/Kutusov, Victor/Oudinot's seniority and disagreement, and local partisan roles at Liakhovo. Wolkowisk retains Reynier's local defence and Schwarzenberg's relief. The two Borisov actions separate Lambert's 21-November assault from Oudinot's 23-November attack against Pahlen II's advance guard, with Chichagov superior. No equal credit follows for every named commander.
- **Full frozen intervals.** The two Krasnoi entries, two Polotsk entries, two Borisov actions, Shevardino/Borodino, and the Riga blockade remain distinct. Riga spans 24 July–18 December, with Essen succeeded by Paulucci and December retreat-order context. Exact appointment dates remain unresolved. November Krasnoi retains Ney's 18–19-November Dnieper crossing without expanding its endpoint to his later reunion.
- **Outcome alternatives.** Chambray characterizes the three days at Jakubovo as producing no result; that disagreement remains beside Bodart’s frozen Russian winner. His following counterfactual is not adopted. Smoliany’s posture is grounded in Boutourlin’s actual 14-November attack, not just the preceding approach.
- **Calendars and real disagreements.** Boutourlin's preface explicitly uses the Russian calendar, 12 days behind Gregorian in 1812. Printed dates are retained, conversions labelled. The offset alone is not a source dispute. Separate unresolved differences remain: Droysen's 22-August Horn/Dahlenkirchen action versus Boutourlin's 11-August Russian-calendar (=23-August Gregorian) Lewis/Grawert Ekau sortie; Chambray's 10-August Swolna versus frozen 11 August; his 9-September Mojaisk occupation versus frozen 10 September; his printed “9 octobre” at Liakhovo in November's narrative and “1er octobre” following late-October Chashniki. None changes the frame.

Original dated local returns and orders remain uninspected; many matched own-side local people totals are still missing, explicitly marked. Retrospective national histories and mediated allies' figures can be historically wrong. Source-backed surprise, intercepted dispatches, marshes, works, food and bridge observations are not invented morale/readiness scores or measured causal effects. Commander-created works/deployments may be mediators. Predictive residuals, tactical effects and campaign contribution remain distinct.
