## Findings and limits

- **Strength populations:** Uclés separates Venegas’s joined force from Infantado’s absent
  reserve. Corunna retains Le Noble’s fewer than 13,000 French bayonets and Oman’s roughly
  15,000 British remaining after embarkation, almost all infantry; Bodart’s cavalry allocation
  is not silently corrected. Ciudad Real separates the advanced cavalry from the assembled
  corps. Maria separates Suchet’s initial fewer than 9,000 from later arrivals and Toreno’s
  at most 12,000 Spaniards present from Areizaga’s detached 5,000. Belchite’s battalions and
  squadrons are not converted into invented headcounts. Talavera keeps British and Spanish
  contingents separate; Ocaña excludes Victor’s distant corps from the facing-force estimate.
  Gerona distinguishes preparation allocations (siege/covering forces), opening garrison,
  reinforced peak, late effective subset and capitulation survivors. No source alternatives
  are averaged or losses mechanically subtracted.
- **Authority:** Corunna records Moore’s message to Hope after his wound without inventing
  an exact receipt clock. Valls separates Souham’s morning action from Saint-Cyr’s combined
  direction. Porto in March retains Toreno’s substitution of Parreiras for the departing bishop,
  alongside Le Noble’s episcopal chief-command description. Puente del Arzobispo distinguishes
  Mortier’s local direction from Soult’s superior dispositions and Bassecourt’s bridge infantry
  from Albuquerque’s ford-watch cavalry. Almonacid starts under Sebastiani before Joseph and
  the reserve arrive. Ocaña distinguishes infantry, cavalry and overall movement direction.
  Gerona separates Verdier’s siege command from the covering army, Augereau’s later arrival
  and Alvarez’s late transfer to Julian Bolivar. These are research observations, not shared
  automatic commander credit.
- **Disputes and clocks:** Le Noble calls the 16 January Corunna field battle a French victory,
  conflicting with Bodart’s British winner; city capitulation on 19 January is separate.
  Le Noble puts the first May Porto crossings in the night of 11–12; Oman describes a midday
  decision and crossing. Jourdan and Toreno put Alba de Tormes on **28 November**, against the
  frozen **26 November**. Toreno’s Valls page is misprinted **373** between 336 and 338;
  IA leaf 342 identifies it. Gerona’s French entry on 11 December is not automatically the
  capitulation date. All frozen dates and winners remain unchanged.
- **Overlapping operations:** La Bisbal’s relief army, vanguard, convoy and French interception
  remain distinct. Its convoy result is not counted again as an independent Gerona victory or
  campaign contribution. The siege and relief narratives do not establish causal commander effects.
- **Missingness and source dependence:** many dated own-side local musters remain unavailable
  within the bounded pass. Braga and March Porto have no inspected original Portuguese return:
  Toreno is Spanish mediation, Le Noble a French opponent account. Jourdan, Suchet and Saint-Cyr
  are interested participant memoirs; Toreno is a retrospective national history; Oman is a
  British synthesis reusing earlier reports and narratives. Embedded reports are not independently
  inspected originals, and agreement may reflect shared material. Historical truth is not proved
  by passage alignment or by reputation.
