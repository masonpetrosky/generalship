# Naples objective-and-result pilot v1

Primary-verified source encoding; no separate review.

**Retain descriptive objective/result components. A common numerical outcome grade is not supported by this pilot.** The existing sources can distinguish local accomplishment from broader expectations, with explicit gaps. Their heterogeneous tasks, criteria and windows do not supply an interchangeable measure of victory quality.

## Scope and coverage

The complete frozen `third-coalition 1806` group is **4 entries / 8 sides**: Campo Tenese, Maida, Gaëta and Amantea. The group is a research/evaluation grouping, not one command appointment. Amantea retains its December 1806–February 1807 interval. The owner accepted this group before the pilot; it is not a representative sample of all 273 development entries.

The [scope](../../data/napoleonic/objective-pilot-v1/scope.json) and [protocol](../../data/napoleonic/objective-pilot-v1/protocol.json) preceded the completed encoding. The [observation record](../../data/napoleonic/objective-pilot-v1/observations.json) retains **19 propositions**, **39 adopted passages**, **26 inspected pinned images** and **4 existing author families**. There are no duplicate entry-side coverage keys. No new source or fetch was required. Reprints, the translated capitulation and multiple pages of a work are not independent witnesses.

The 8 prior objective claims and all 49 citation indices remain accounted for: 22 cite an adopted excerpt or containing context; 27 are unchanged prior-reference-only pointers, not newly verified propositions. The bound dossiers, prepared packet, raw files and prior measurement/model records remain unchanged.

For **7/8 sides**, a source reports an intention before the relevant action; **1/8** retains only an inferred disposition. Gaëta B also has a separate late-siege council intention before further resistance. Its early refusal, general royal/regency command and later council are not collapsed into one timeless mandate. **0/8** has an adopted objective supported by a supplied, contemporaneously dated pre-action document. This concerns the inspected passages, not the survival of all possible archival evidence. Retrospective reporting may support descriptive history; it does not establish when an intention was recorded or known.

Explicit success criteria remain null for **8/19 propositions**. **33/39** adopted passages were checked on pinned images; the other **6/39** are explicitly OCR-only. Two full-OCR quotations about Damas’s partial junction were checked on the existing p. 396 image; that paragraph is absent from the old partial figure transcription, which stays unchanged.

## Interpretation and feasible use

**Local results can be recorded without a universal score.** Koch’s reported directive to attack the left corps at Campo Tenese can be compared with his account of an attack. At Gaëta and Amantea, a reported purpose to take the named place can be linked to surrender/occupation. These are different kinds of accomplishment, not equally valuable successes or estimates of command contribution.

**Partial results and prior-objective uncertainty matter.** Damas describes retirement from Campo Tenese and some joining at the Crati, with the advanced guard not joining. The planned delay is not measured. His later “inch by inch” judgment is commentary, not a prior mandate. At Amantea, fidelity language and resistance do not supply the garrison’s prior operational objective or deadline; its side remains in the table.

**Maida’s battlefield result does not fulfill the whole expected chain.** Bunbury’s anticipated retreat toward Catanzaro has a qualified directional match in the reported retreat; arrival timing and causation are unestablished. The general-insurrection criterion remains undefined. The anticipated relief of Gaëta through abandonment is not observed by the later capitulation/entry. This linked evidence is not another Maida win or an extra Gaëta outcome weight. Movement from the original French position also prevents treating victory as proof of the planned camp attack/flank geometry.

**A siege’s eventual surrender does not answer every mission.** The late Gaëta council favors holding longer, but the OCR duration `dix4iait jours*` has no pinned p. 243 facsimile in the allowed set. Its numeric duration stays null; no deadline or mission-failure label is invented. Colletta’s early higher-command refusal and Koch’s speculative origin account remain distinct. Hotz/Storz and superior/delegated roles are not reconciled into one exclusive commander.

**Capture, speed, redeployment and pacification stay separate.** A surrender does not establish how much a siege was accelerated or that the entire reported besieging body was used elsewhere. Amantea’s reduction does not prove wider pacification: the source presents a necessary condition involving more than one town, not sufficiency. Conditional embarkation/non-service terms do not establish permanent force destruction or later compliance. Koch’s 33 siege days and Colletta’s 40 excluding the first assault keep their different clocks; no efficiency ratio is calculated.

These are material measurement limits for a common grade, rather than reasons to discard useful descriptive passages. No common utility scale, defensible aggregation, complete appointment window, replacement pool or causal identification is supplied here. Source agreement is not historical adjudication; the translated outcome instrument remains a printed version in Koch’s family. The OCR-only passages remain listed below, with no correction or claim of unseen facsimile inspection.

## Decision and stop

**Go for retaining these source-qualified descriptive components; no-go for deriving one common numerical grade from them.** No scalar, ordinal rank, commander credit, model input or historical fit is produced. All 52 final entries remain unused numerically. The strict-opening, identity and 1805 appointment/window branches keep their existing stopped/paused dispositions. This pilot ends with the table and decision; no wider extraction, new source search or run follows automatically.
