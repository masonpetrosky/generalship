## Inspection and stopping record

Read all eleven frozen battle, force and commander row sets and the ten retained NPS pages in
full. NPS/CWSAC and the Arnold tables are one family. The MO022 (Glasgow) NPS detail page is an
empty template; it is registered as a diagnostic snapshot, and the frozen Arnold row and its
description stand for the family there. Every record uses the same two further families.

- **Britton, *The Civil War on the Border*, volume II** (Putnam, 1899; copyright 1898) is a
  retrospective history by a Union participant. Its preface says the larger operations are based
  mainly on the published official reports, so it is not independent of the Price family. The
  University of California scan of the 1899 printing was used; an NYPL scan whose title page
  reads 1904 was not used. The OCR was byte-identical to the parent already registered by the
  Camden pass (`britton-civil-war-border-2-ocr-v1`), so the selection points to that parent.

    (pp.489–504);

## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** The following are context or separate records, and none is added to another record:
  - the September 26 Arcadia Valley skirmishes and Ewing's retreat to Leasburg;
  - the October 15 capture of Sedalia and the surrenders of Paris and Carrollton;
  - the evening fighting in Independence on October 21;
  - the October 22 fight between Shelby and Kansas militia south of Byram's Ford;
  - the Little Osage action between Mine Creek and the Marmiton;
  - the siege of Fayetteville and the pursuit to the Arkansas.

  MO026's frozen interval covers Shelby's forcing of the Big Blue on the 22nd and Pleasonton's
  forcing of Byram's Ford on the 23rd; the result field is kept for both days. MO028 follows
  Britton and Price in taking the evening stand in front of Price's unarmed men as the Marmiton
  (Charlot's farm, Shiloh Creek) action. Where it happened is disputed: NPS puts it at the
  Marmiton ford, and Britton on Shiloh Creek about six miles northeast of Fort Scott, before the
  retreat to the Marmiton crossing. Price does not name the place; identifying his "prairie
  beyond the river" line with Britton's Shiloh Creek stand is an interpretation, and part of his
  account may cover the Little Osage action.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.**
  - Frozen side-specific bounds exist only for MO022: US 800 ("Garrison (800)"). All the others
    are blank.
  - Every live NPS "Forces Engaged" field reads zero, which is not measured absence.
  - No figure is adopted. Each figure below is recorded with its date and scope:
    - Price: on entering Missouri on September 19, nearly 8,000 armed and 4,000 unarmed men
      (nearly 12,000 with fourteen guns);
    - Price's intelligence estimates: Ironton about 1,500; Blunt, Lane and Jennison 3,000 to
      4,000 at Lexington; Blunt's army on the Big Blue 6,000 to 8,000;
    - Price at Westport: more than 20,000 against fewer than 8,000 armed;
    - Price: Marmaduke's report of 3,000 pursuers before Mine Creek, and Blunt's 3,000 cavalry
      at Newtonia;
    - Britton at Pilot Knob: Ewing's 489 and 562 men, 1,051 in all, plus about 100 citizens, and
      5,600 Confederates in the assault;
    - Britton: at Glasgow, Kirkham's estimate of 5,000 to 7,000 under Clark against 750 at most;
    - Britton at Westport: 7,000 to 8,000 under Blunt, with 3,000 to 4,000 militia left in the
      Kansas City works;
    - NPS: Blunt's 2,000 at Lexington and the Little Blue.
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - Strength:
    - Fort Davidson: Price's 1,500 against Britton's 1,051 plus citizens;
    - Glasgow: frozen 800 against Britton's 750;
    - Lexington: 2,000 against 3,000–4,000;
    - Little Blue: each side is said to have had superior numbers;
    - Westport: over 20,000 against 7,000–8,000;
    - Newtonia: five brigades against two small brigades or 3,000.
  - Result:
    - Independence: the frozen and live results read Confederate victory, but Price and Britton
      describe Cabell's brigade broken and Marmaduke driven back;
    - Westport: Price says he drove the Federals six or seven miles into Westport before
      withdrawing to protect his train;
    - Newtonia: Price says Blunt was repulsed three miles; Britton says Sanborn's arrival
      saved Blunt.
  - Newtonia information: surprise (NPS) against warning (Price, Britton).
  - Fort Davidson bombardment: the frozen and NPS description says Price, considering the time
    involved, dismissed mounting guns on the high ground; Britton says Price concurred in
    Mackey's advice to bombard, and the council chose assault because the garrison was
    reported to be largely colored soldiers and some officers wished to avenge Ewing.
  - Casualties:
    - Fort Davidson: frozen 1,684 against live 1,800, and Ewing's 200 against 1,500 and
      Mackey's 1,164;
    - Glasgow: frozen 450 against Harding's 11 killed and 32 wounded and Greene's 7 and 46;
    - Independence: 140 against 300–400 cut off and 400 prisoners;
    - Mine Creek: frozen 1,300 against live 950; NPS 600 prisoners against Price's 300–400
      and Britton's more than 400 in Philips's front;
    - Newtonia: frozen 650 against live 50.
- **Command roles and ranks.** No listed commander receives automatic sole credit, and no live
  rank is adopted.
  - The live pages give Blunt as Brigadier General (MO023, MO026, MO029) and Shelby and Fagan
    as Brigadier General.
  - Price's report styles Fagan and Marmaduke Major General. The frozen table gives Marmaduke
    as Major General for KS004 but Brigadier General for KS003, MO025 and MO026.
  - NPS names McNeil "John S. McNeil".
- **Tags.** No works or ground are tagged `inherited`. Fort Davidson predates Ewing, but he added
  rifle-pits, and the Big Blue obstructions were made by the defenders. All claims stay
  `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Campaign contribution.** The following are campaign context, not tactical outcome:
  - Price's recruiting and his claimed captures and destruction;
  - the loss of his train;
  - the dispute over the Mine Creek trophies;
  - Pleasonton's withdrawal and the recall of the Missouri troops.

The eleven null unknowns are the eleven opening strengths. No morale/readiness score,
probability, causal effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The cohort, both admission
proposals and the baseline are unchanged.
