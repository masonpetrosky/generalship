summaries in full. NPS/CWSAC and the Arnold tables are one family.

Each battle uses NPS/CWSAC and two further families:

- **Greene**, *The Mississippi* (1882), reused through one new selection from the pinned

- **Snyder's Bluff, Milliken's Bend and Goodrich's Landing** use both sides' reports in

Letters and telegrams quoted inside a selected report are part of that report, not
separate families. No maps, compiled returns, navy reports or print pages were

## Decisions and limits

- **Nesting.** MS011's frozen siege interval (May 18–July 4) contains Milliken's Bend,
  Goodrich's Landing and Helena, which are separate engagements. Greene's figure of a
  little under 10,000 Union losses covers the whole campaign and is not assigned to the
  siege; the live MS011 Confederate figure (32,697) appears to include the surrendered
  garrison. No figure is adopted.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown** for all ten; every frozen side-specific bound is
  blank. Reported figures differ in scope, for example Bowen's 5,500 against Greene's
  returns of 8,500 at Port Gibson, Gregg's 2,500 against Greene's "over 3,000" at
  Raymond, McCulloch's 1,500 against Dennis's 2,500 at Milliken's Bend, and Holmes's
  7,646 against the Union estimate of 15,000 at Helena.
- **Disputes preserved**: Goodrich's Landing's surrender, which NPS calls unconditional
  and dates to June 29, while Walker describes prisoner-of-war terms for the officers and
  unconditional surrender for the Black soldiers, dated June 30; Jackson's evacuation
  clocks (noon, 2 p.m., mid-afternoon); vessel counts at Snyder's Bluff; and casualty
  figures in every record, including Raymond's three Confederate totals (569, 514, 505)
  and Port Gibson's three Union totals (861, 875, 849).
- **Command roles and ranks**: judgments are attributed to their authors: Pemberton on
  the Big Black defenders and on why he surrendered, Greene on Johnston and on the
  garrison's temper, Holmes on Walker, Price and McRae, and NPS on the Jackson
  evacuation. Live headings rank Grant Lieutenant General, Johnston Lieutenant Colonel,
  Sherman General and Prentiss Brigadier General against the frozen ranks; none is
  adopted, and no listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Goodrich's Landing's purpose** is recorded from Walker's report: breaking up leased
  plantations and returning about 2,000 captured Black people to enslavement. The fate
  of the soldiers surrendered unconditionally remains a deferred question.
- Pre-existing works at Grand Gulf, Big Black, Vicksburg, the Goodrich's Landing mound
  and Helena are tagged `inherited` relative to each engagement start; other terrain,
  logistics and command claims stay `unresolved`.

The ten null unknowns are the opening strengths. No morale/readiness score,
