## Decisions and limits

- **Scope and overlaps.**
  - Deep Bottom II (VA071, August 13–20) and Globe Tavern (VA072, August 18–21) overlap.
  - Chaffin's Farm (VA075) and Peebles' Farm (VA074) meet on September 30.
  - Boydton Plank Road (VA079) and Fair Oaks (VA080) are simultaneous.
  - Each dossier names the related records.

  Totals that span more than one record are not assigned to one record:
  - the Wilson-Kautz raid's wide totals (Wilson's about 900; Lee's over 1,000 prisoners; the
    frozen "1,817 for entire raid");
  - Hampton's June 28–30 figures;
  - Humphreys' army-wide returns.

  Context only:
  - the June 16–17 Bermuda Hundred fighting;
  - the July 30 diversion effect of Deep Bottom I;
  - the Stony Creek wagon haul after Globe Tavern;
  - the March 25 picket-line captures that preceded April 2.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Frozen numeric side bounds exist only for three records:
  - VA063 (US 62,000; CS 42,000);
  - VA083 (US 34,517; CS 13,835);
  - VA098 (US 4,500 only; the Confederate row carries "2,500" as text without bounds).

  The other records have blank bounds, with undivided totals for VA113, VA072 and VA079. Every
  figure found is recorded with its date, basis and scope, and none is adopted, among them:
  - Humphreys' June 9 components (1,800, 1,200 and 1,500, which match the frozen Union 4,500)
    and Wise's 2,400.
  - For June 15–18: Smith's 10,000 with 2,400 and 3,700; the Second Corps' 20,000; Johnson's
    3,500; and Beauregard's probable 14,000 on June 16.
  - The raid's 5,500 cavalry and Wilson's 500–600 bridge defenders; Birge's 2,500.
  - The July returns (37,984 and 39,295) and the August effective infantry (33,684 and 18,449).
  - The signal officers' 8,000–10,000.
  - Ord's 4,000, Birney's 10,000, Stannard's field return of 3,115 less 260, and Bratton's
    1,165 men and 129 officers.
  - Kautz's 1,700.
  - The Boydton plan of 30,000–35,000 infantry: Hancock's 10,000, and Parke's and Warren's
    11,000 each.
  - The October 20 Confederate return (13,638 infantry and 5,453 cavalry).
  - The December 20 return (3,609 officers and 50,155 men).
  - The spring 1865 returns (not less than 46,000 against 69,000).
  - Gordon's "about one-half of the Army" at Fort Stedman.
- **Disputes preserved** (37 claims marked `disputed`: nine force-scope, nine result and nineteen
  casualty claims). Among them:
  - **Frozen against live casualties.**
    - Petersburg, June 15–18: 11,386 against 7,674. Humphreys' 8,150 Union killed and wounded
      matches the frozen US figure but is his estimate.
    - Jerusalem Plank Road: 4,000 against 3,534.
    - Crater: 5,300 against 5,289.
    - Deep Bottom II: 4,600 against 4,200.
    - Globe Tavern: 5,879 against 6,055.
    - Reams's Station, August 25: 3,492 against 3,556.
    - Chaffin's Farm: 4,430 against 5,000.
    - Peebles' Farm: 3,800 against 4,169.
    - October 7: 1,750 against 1,158.
    - October 13: 950 against 487.
    - Fair Oaks: 1,750 against 1,703.
    - Hatcher's Run: 2,700 against 2,539.
    - Fort Stedman: 3,850 (US 950) against 3,698 (US 1,017). Parke's 1,017 matches the live
      figure.
    - The live zeros on the raid records and on June 9 and Deep Bottom I.
  - **Result categories.** For Jerusalem Plank Road and Hatcher's Run the frozen record reads
    "Union gained ground" and the live record "Union Victory"; the two are not reconciled.
  - **Commanders' characterizations.**
    - Sappony Church: Wilson's repulses against Hampton's rout.
    - Staunton River: Lee's "signally repulsed" against Wilson's claim that the raid's objects
      were in the main accomplished.
    - Globe Tavern: Lee's "Defeated" against the recorded Union victory.
    - Peebles' Farm: the Union gains against Heth's repulse of Potter.
    - Boydton: Mahone "swept from the field" against the Union withdrawal.
  - **Counts.**
    - Crater: Burnside's 170 Fourth Division killed against 176 in Humphreys; Meade's 4,400
      against the Medical table's 4,008 and Humphreys' probable 3,500.
    - Reams's Station: Hill's 2,150 prisoners and 12 colors against Lee's 2,000 and seven.
    - Reams's Station, June 29: Wilson's reports identify the infantry as Hoke's and as
      Finegan's brigade of Mahone's division.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - Rank differences:
    - The live pages give "Major General Robert Lee" on eight records; the frozen rows have
      General.
    - The June 9 page gives Gillmore and Beauregard as Brigadier General.
    - The live pages give Hampton as Lieutenant General on VA067, and Terry as Brigadier General
      and Anderson as Major General on VA078.
    - The live VA080 page gives Longstreet as Major General.
    - The frozen rows give Wilson as Brigadier General on VA067 and VA113 but Major General on
      VA068.
  - The live VA068 page lists Hancock and Heth, the commanders of the August 25 battle. None of
    these rank or commander fields is adopted.
  - Quoted directions and criticisms are attributed as the sources give them:
    - Butler's indorsement calling June 9 "disgraceful", and Gillmore's reply;
    - Smith's halt on June 15 and Humphreys' counterfactual;
    - Meade's fixing of the June 18 hour;
    - Birney's unsecured flank on June 22;
    - Burnside's rejected plan, Ledlie's absence and Meade's request for Burnside's relief;
    - Hancock's criticism of Barlow;
    - the conditions imposed on Warren;
    - Longstreet's anticipation at Fair Oaks;
    - Parke's command at Fort Stedman in Meade's absence.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit. The direction of some listed commanders is
    not described in the inspected passages: Butler at Chaffin's Farm, Terry and Anderson on
    October 13, Birney on October 7 and Field at Deep Bottom I.
- **Tags.** Fifteen terrain claims are tagged `inherited`: pre-existing works, woods, swamps,
  creeks and rivers. One claim is tagged `commander_created`: the crater made by the Union mine,
  which became an obstacle to the attackers. Works built during an engagement stay
  `unresolved`. Outcomes are `post_outcome`.
- **OCR limits.**
  - The inferred page locators are listed above.
  - Section dates are left null for the Gillmore report, the Hancock report, Lee's October
    correction and Lee's second February dispatch.
  - Garbled numbers are not read: Lee's correction gives "0 guns" and his October 28 dispatch
    "0 pieces of artillery".
  - Quotes preserve OCR corruption, for example "Dealing’s" for Dearing's, "Kearns’ Station",
    "wThole" and "Our foss".
