## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** The five records are separate days. Proctor's Creek (VA053, May 12–16) includes
  Whiting's Petersburg column of May 16, which was part of Beauregard's plan.
  - The Fort Clifton gunboat action of May 9 appears in the VA050 frozen description.
  - Kautz's raids, the June 2 fight and the late-May detachments to Lee are context.
  - The frozen VA054 description credits the May victories with enabling reinforcements for Lee.
    That is campaign contribution, not the record's result.
  - Army-wide returns, Butler's May 5–31 return and the multi-day totals are not assigned to one
    record.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** Only VA053 has frozen numeric side bounds (US 30,000;
  CS 18,000). The others have blank bounds with force text 'Divisions', and VA054 has an
  undivided 10,000 total. Every figure found is recorded with its date, basis and scope, and
  none is adopted:
  - Humphreys' Army of the James figures: 1,329 officers and 30,543 enlisted infantry and 82
    guns, built from corps figures he gives as present for duty; Kautz's cavalry as 97 officers
    and 2,804 enlisted men.
  - His Confederate reconstructions: about 6,000 infantry at Richmond on May 1; 19,000 infantry
    (enlisted men present for duty) in the arriving brigades; 22,000 infantry and 2,000 cavalry
    by May 15; not less than 17,000 in the three Drury's Bluff divisions (about 5,400, 7,000 and
    4,900); Whiting's not less than 4,600.
  - Butler's 1,700 and 3,000 cavalry.
  - Hagood's aggregate of 1,500 engaged on May 7 (no "about" in the passage), and Johnson's
    800 muskets.
  - The post-detachment figures: Butler's 10,000 infantry; Smith's nearly 16,000; Beauregard's
    about 9,000.
  - The live VA053 force field (57,000; US 39,000) against its own description's 30,000.
- **Disputes preserved** (14 claims marked `disputed`: five force-scope, four result and five
  casualty claims):
  - **Port Walthall Junction.** The frozen and live records say a Union division drove the
    brigades from the depot and cut the railroad. Humphreys says the May 7 force met the enemy
    "without any important result either way". Hagood says the enemy withdrew from the field.
    The frozen 550 casualties stand against the live zero and Hagood's own 177 with his
    estimate of 1,000 for the enemy.
  - **Swift Creek.** Butler says he "whipped" the enemy, against the recorded inconclusive
    result.
  - **Chester Station.** Humphreys says "only some skirmishing ensued". Barton describes a
    close fight in burning woods at 20–50 paces, ending when he was outflanked.
  - **Proctor's Creek losses.** The frozen figure is 6,660 and the live figure 4,004. Beauregard
    reports 2,184 lost on May 16 and claims 1,400 prisoners. Badeau gives 3,500 Union losses on
    May 16 only. The prisoner list is incomplete and its garbled row appears to end 43, 1,345,
    1,388.
  - **Ware Bottom Church.** The live and frozen 'Confederate victory' stands against Humphreys'
    account of the pits retaken on Terry's front. Humphreys gives a loss of 702 killed and
    wounded, and an enemy loss "equally great".
  - The live zeros for forces and casualties on VA047, VA050, VA051 and VA054 are not measured
    absence.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live pages give Beauregard as Brigadier General; the frozen rows have General. The live
    rank is not adopted.
  - Quoted directions are attributed as the sources give them:
    - Butler's rejection of the Petersburg proposal and his order for Gillmore to fall back;
    - Beauregard's plan, his withholding of Ransom and his blame of the left wing and
      Whiting, with the June 14 letter on the left-wing commander;
    - Davis's partial approval of the plan;
    - Hagood reporting to Bushrod Johnson;
    - Barton's formation under Ransom's orders and his relief, in Barton's own defensive
      account.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** Two terrain claims are tagged `inherited`: the Port Walthall sunken road and
  embankment, and the Swift Creek line. The Chester Station ridge and morass and the Drury's
  Bluff intrenchments and fog were tagged `inherited` in the first pass; after the review
  correction they are `unresolved`, because each also contains conditions that arose during the
  action (the Union fortifying, the woods fire and the close range; the fog of May 16). The May
  20 rifle-pits and the Howlett Line stay `unresolved`. Outcomes are `post_outcome`.
- **OCR limits.**
  - Page locators rely on OCR running heads; p.137 is inferred.
  - Several quotes preserve OCR corruption, for example "Our foss was 177", "ihadanaggre eate of
    1 500 men engaged", "cokinel" and "Whiting s gups".
  - Beauregard's casualty and prisoner lists and his May 16–17 telegrams are garbled. Only legible
    phrases are quoted, and no column assignment is inferred.

The eight null unknowns are:

- information at Ware Bottom Church.
