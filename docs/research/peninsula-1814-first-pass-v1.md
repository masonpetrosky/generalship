## What the evidence preserves

- **Orthez strength alternatives.** Bodart gives French/allied 35,000/45,000. Lapène gives at most 32,000 French before deployment, then fewer than 35,000 against 70,000 in his battle summary. Oman gives about 36,000 French with components adding to 36,500, and allied components adding to about 42,800. His separately arriving 3,750 conscripts are not an opening reinforcement assumed present or engaged. Rounded estimates, opponent estimates, components and internal arithmetic remain distinct.
- **Toulouse dates and populations.** Bodart's French/allied 32,000/60,000 is separate from Lapène's 21,000 French first line and 6,000 second line. Oman's French states are explicitly **April 1**, nine days before the battle: 42,043 across broader reserve/service categories. His allied appendix prints 49,146 but qualifies cavalry presence and cannot give an exact local engineer/train count because the returns include Bayonne and Bordeaux. The Spanish sappers inside his infantry category are preserved as printed, not silently reclassified.
- **Bayonne opening, sortie and closure.** Bodart's garrison/investors 14,000/25,000 differs in population and time from Oman's late-February investing distribution, 15,000 north plus 16,000 south. Bodart's 3,000 sortie force and Oman's 6,000 remain alternatives. Oman's April 26 garrison under arms is after the frozen endpoint. Bodart ends the siege April 18; Lapène describes instructions six days after the last success (about April 20 if counted from the April 14 sortie), while Oman dates the official order/flag change April 26. The relative-date calculation is ours, not a dated receipt. None replaces the frozen interval.
- **Command and information.** Soult/Wellington's superior roles remain distinct from Reille, Beresford, Hill, Freire, Travot and other described local tasks. At Bayonne Thouvenot's governorship differs from Abbé/Maucomble's sortie direction. Hope's capture, subordinate counterattacks and Colville's later succession are retained without an invented exact replacement clock. Reported warnings and knowledge remain attributed, not complete contemporary information sets. Oman's and Lapène's evaluations of the sortie differ; neither is adjudicated by reputation.
- **Posture and works.** The allied offensives at Orthez/Toulouse and Bayonne's investment are separate from French intentions, defensive counterattacks and the later sortie. Toulouse's advance construction orders are attributed to Soult; arriving before the army does not automatically make the works an inherited external advantage. Bayonne's opening French works and later works of both sides retain different phase/agency limits. Orthez's bounded passages leave military fortification unknown; natural ridges are not entrenchments.
