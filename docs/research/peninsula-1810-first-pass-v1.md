## Findings and limits

- **Source populations differ.** Vich retains Belmas’s 15,000 Spanish advancing from Moya, Toreno’s 8,000 infantry plus 1,000 cavalry before the approach, and Bodart’s 12,000 available. Margalef’s departing 6,000 infantry/600 cavalry is separate from Lerida’s garrison. Lerida retains Toreno’s 8,000 including Perena against Bodart’s 9,000. Mequinenza retains Toreno’s printed 1,200 against Bodart’s 1,800. Tortosa retains 7,179 against 11,000. These alternatives are not averaged or declared equivalent dated musters.
- **Almeida and Busaco.** Belmas’s about 6,000 Almeida defenders are distinct from Oman’s about 4,000 infantry plus a cavalry squadron and 400 gunners. Busaco retains Marbot’s 50,000 all-arms invasion-force scope against Bodart’s 58,000. Oman’s printed allied table totals 52,272, but includes two unseparated absent Portuguese batteries; the narrative says 52,000 on the field. No absent battery count is invented. Bodart’s 32,000 stays frozen. Original Portuguese returns remain uninspected.
- **Local versus superior authority.** Astorga separates Clausel’s initial investment from Junot’s corps command. Margalef separates Harispe/Musnier’s local tasks from Suchet’s direction. Ciudad Rodrigo and Almeida separate Ney’s investment, Junot’s support and Masséna’s superior role. Tortosa retains Toreno’s accusation of partial Alacha/Uriarte delegation without inventing a replacement clock. Marbot’s retrospective allocation of blame is attributed, not accepted as causal commander skill.
- **Full Cádiz interval.** Alburquerque’s early arrival/departure force of 9,000 is separate from Toreno’s late-March 14,000–15,000 Spanish army and Bodart’s multi-year allied summary. Early Alburquerque command, Victor in May 1811 and Soult’s 1812 withdrawal direction do not establish a continuous appointment chain. Toreno describes withdrawal beginning 24 August and the line clear on the 25th. Adjacent Barrosa/Chiclana field action is not counted again as a siege victory or campaign contribution.
- **Phases and missingness.** Existing permanent works, new transport/siege arrangements, and unresolved construction authorship remain distinct. French investing counts as offensive posture under brief v3; this does not assert a successful assault. Vich’s 19 versus frozen 20 February sequence remains unresolved. Exact local strengths, superior instructions, naval support, army identity and replacement times remain incomplete. Source agreement can reflect dependence; source alignment is not historical truth.
