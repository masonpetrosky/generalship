Prepared 2026-09-20. **Research packet awaiting separate review. No exact first

The [research record](../../design/shiloh-contact-location-v1/research-record.json)

## What the accounts establish, and what they dispute

These are reported events, not a harmonized clock. Report dates below describe
composition, never event time or when a commander received information. All
historical knowledge timestamps remain null.

The separately pinned Beauregard report, April 11, p.386, reports contact at
5 a.m. and movement at 5:30 (CL13). It is an army-level account, not evidence
that there were no earlier qualifying shots. No averaging, ranking of clocks by
precision, or conversion of dawn/sunrise to an invented timestamp is performed.

Reed's pp.13, 59–60 and 68 name Powell, give **4:55 a.m.**, and place contact
near the southeast corner of Fraley field / the corner of Fraley's and Wood's
fields (CL11). Reed then describes withdrawal toward Seay Field. This is a
later reconstruction that draws on the reports; repeated 4:55 statements within
it are not independent corroboration. Its exact-minute derivation is untraced
here. Reed also describes April 3 cavalry skirmishing and a Saturday engagement
near Howell's (CL02); the underlying reports remain research leads.

**Boundary consequence:** retain the v2 April 6 proposal as an immutable
proposal, with its explicit earlier-contact caveat. This packet neither adopts
3 a.m., 4:55, 5 a.m., dawn or 5:30 as B0 nor moves B0 to April 4. First define
whether the relevant engagement includes the earlier encounters; then identify
the earliest event under that definition. Recorded CWSAC dates do not settle
the historical segmentation question.

## Maps and relative positions

The [Union manuscript](https://www.loc.gov/item/2006636339/) distinguishes blue
morning positions from red night positions and puts north to the right. The
catalog's [1862] date comes from Stephenson's 1989 catalog, rather than a proven
pre-battle preparation date. The full-resolution image and the exact catalog
JSON response are retained as `loc-2006636339-map-v1` and `-catalog-v1`.
Selected labels/legend readings are `loc-2006636339-readings-v1`.

Blue lines identify Sherman, Prentiss, McClernand, Hurlbut, W. H. L. Wallace and
Stuart. They are **morning**, not necessarily immediately pre-contact. Red Lew
Wallace and Buell positions and red river gunboat symbols cannot be imported
into the morning state. The map provides relative roads, camps/lines, creeks and
landing context, but no complete outpost or personnel inventory.

The [Frémaux map endorsed by Beauregard](https://www.loc.gov/item/85690890/)
is titled for April 6–7 together. Its legend distinguishes Confederate and
Federal symbols, U.S. camps, gunboats and headquarters, without an opening
clock. It labels Hardee, Bragg, Polk and Breckinridge's lines and Tanner and
Greer fords. The label readings and corresponding full image are retained under
`loc-85690890-*-v1`. Its endorsement is a reporting-chain link, not independent
corroboration of Beauregard's account. Neither catalog year establishes that
the map was available to a commander before contact.

| River, transports and naval crews | Map boat/landing symbols establish a cartographic question | No inspected deck logs or embarkation/disembarkation census; no boat symbol proves opening crew membership |

The symmetric physical-availability policy remains a proposal. **The afloat rule
is unresolved:** define the water/bank boundary and recover vessel/personnel
locations before counting or excluding combat crews or transport passengers.
Being afloat, at a landing, in reserve or not yet firing does not itself establish
exclusion. Map scale bars do not justify a modern georeferenced perimeter without
control points and uncertainty. `area` and both complete membership sets stay null.
