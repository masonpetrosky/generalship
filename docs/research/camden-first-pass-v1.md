## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.**
  - Elkin's Ferry's frozen record also carries the name Okolona. The April 2 rear-guard fight near
    Okolona and Terre Noir Creek and the April 4 picket fight at Okolona are recorded separately,
    and their figures are not added to the ferry fight.
  - Prairie D'Ane's interval (April 9–13) includes the April 10–12 fighting on the prairie and the
    April 13 action at Moscow, recorded as one record without summing figures.
  - Context only:
    - the march from Little Rock (from March 23);
    - Thayer's junction;
    - the march into Camden (April 14–15);
    - the capture of the steamer Homer;
    - the April 23 feint on Camden;
    - the evacuation of Camden;
    - the April 29 rear-guard skirmishes;
    - the retreat to Little Rock.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The only frozen bound is Poison Spring's Union 1,100, which
  the live page repeats; no inspected passage gives its basis. Every other bound is blank. Every
  figure is recorded with its date and scope, and none is adopted:
  - Price: Steele's column estimated at 10,000 with 25 guns (March 23), and some 1,200 Confederates
    actually engaged at Elkin's Ferry.
  - Britton:
    - about twelve thousand for Steele once Thayer joined;
    - Price's district about twelve thousand;
    - Shelby's fifteen hundred on April 2.
  - Prairie D'Ane:
    - Thayer's division 5,500 (Price) or about five thousand (Britton);
    - Confederate reinforcements: Gano's 500 (with Marmaduke on the prairie by April 7) and
      Tandy Walker's about 1,000 (with Maxey before the April 13 Moscow attack);
    - Thayer's rear guard some 3,000 at Moscow (Price).
  - Poison Spring:
    - Britton: escort 695, plus Duncan's 383 and 90 cavalry (not summed here); attackers
      thirty-seven hundred;
    - Price: about 1,500 guarding the train.
  - Marks' Mills:
    - Britton: a brigade, 400 cavalry and four guns, against Fagan's four thousand, four to one;
    - Price: a brigade, 500 cavalry and six guns.
  - Jenkins' Ferry:
    - Britton: Steele not more than 4000 on the field; Kirby Smith nearly 10,000, derived from an
      assumed 1,000 per brigade; the three Louisiana divisions about eight thousand on arrival.
- **Disputes preserved.**
  - Elkin's Ferry:
    - frozen 92 against live 55;
    - Price's account of the Union being driven back three miles, against Britton's repulse of
      Marmaduke;
    - the frozen three Confederate brigades against Britton's two.
  - Prairie D'Ane: the frozen casualty text Unknown against live 150, with no inspected figure.
  - Poison Spring:
    - 198 wagons (Britton, NPS) against some 225 (Price);
    - Britton's Union 122 killed, 97 wounded and 81 missing, and Maxey's incomplete 17 and 88;
    - Price's near 500 Union dead;
    - Price's report of Williams's death, flagged as a mistake by the compiler.
  - Marks' Mills:
    - 211, 240 or "several hundred" wagons;
    - Drake severely or mortally wounded;
    - Fagan's 150 against Cabell's over 200 wounded;
    - Drake's report, relayed by Britton: his chief surgeon's count of about 250 of his men
      killed and wounded, and an estimated 800–900 dead and wounded on the field, about half of
      them Confederates and many of the rest refugees and Black civilians with the train.
  - Jenkins' Ferry:
    - frozen 964 against live 1,700;
    - Britton's Union 86 killed, 500 wounded and 61 missing;
    - Cunningham's Confederate 800;
    - incomplete returns of 99 killed and 437 wounded for Price's divisions;
    - Price's account of the enemy yielding the ground, against Britton's of an unmolested
      withdrawal.
- **Killing of Black soldiers and civilians.** At Poison Spring, Britton reports Confederate troops
  shooting wounded Black soldiers, Black soldiers cut off from their command shot down without
  mercy, and Cabell's report of one regiment killing at least 80. At Marks' Mills he relays
  Drake's report that many of the dead were refugees and Black civilians travelling with the
  train, who had been murdered. These are recorded as reported, and the dossiers note that such
  deaths are not ordinary combat categories and that civilian dead are not military casualties.
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - The live pages list Marmaduke without a rank and give Kirby Smith as Lieutenant General
    (frozen General).
  - At Poison Spring, Maxey commanded the combined force as senior brigadier.
  - At Jenkins' Ferry, Kirby Smith had assumed command and Price led two infantry divisions.
  - No inspected passage places Steele on the field at Elkin's Ferry.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit, and live ranks are not adopted.
- **Tags.** The Confederate works at Prairie D'Ane predate the April 12 advance, but their timing
  is not established. All claims stay `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Campaign contribution.** The following are recorded as campaign context, not tactical
  outcome:
  - Steele's turn from Shreveport toward Camden;
  - the abandonment of the junction with Banks after Marks' Mills;
  - the evacuation of Camden.

  NPS's statement that the Confederates bungled a chance at Jenkins' Ferry is a counterfactual
  judgment.
- **Families.** Britton is placed in `britton-civil-war-border-1899` and Price in
  `price-camden-1864-report`. Neither author had an earlier registry group.

The five null unknowns are the five opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability,
causal effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The cohort, both admission proposals and the
baseline are unchanged, with **zero promoted rows**.

## Review correction

Each finding was checked against the retained selection text before any change; every new
quote occurs in its section, and the dossiers were regenerated from the builder. All five
dossiers are revised under `camden-review-correction-2026-09-25` (the Britton note in the
common open questions touches each of them) and supersede byte-for-byte archives at
`data/evidence/history/AR012.v1.json` to `AR016.v1.json`. Both required findings for this
campaign hold and are applied:

- **RRC-5** (AR013 `reported-force-scope`): Gano's and Tandy Walker's brigades were Confederate
  reinforcements (Gano's to Marmaduke on the prairie; Walker's with Maxey before the April 13
  Moscow attack), not reinforcements of Steele. Value corrected; the two Price citations are
  lengthened to include Marmaduke and Maxey's command (p.780).
- **RRC-6** (AR015 `casualty-records`): the 250 and 800–900 figures are Colonel Drake's report,
  relayed by Britton, and Drake said about half of the dead and wounded on the field were
  Confederates. Value corrected; two citations added (pp.294–295). Civilian dead remain outside
  military casualties.

Advisories:

- **Adopted as wording and citations:** RRA-4 (AR012 `casualty-records`: Price gives no date
  for Shelby's Terre Noir attack; April 2 is Britton's; Price's location and Britton's date
  cited), RRA-5 (AR014 `casualty-records`: "wounded and cut-off" replaces "wounded and
  captured" in the rationale; Britton's "shot down without mercy" sentence added to the value
  and cited).
- **Partly adopted:** RRA-8 (Britton as partly a participant's interested Union account, and the
  *National Tribune* serialization). Recorded in the dossiers' common open question and in this
  memo from the preface. The registry dependency note is not revised: the Britton volume II
  parent `britton-civil-war-border-2-ocr-v1` is shared with a later pass, and a metadata-only
  successor for the selection alone would diverge from it. Left for a later metadata-only
  revision.

Citations rise from 226 to 231; claims (45) and unknowns (5) are unchanged, and no claim's
status changes. No source record is added or changed. No model input, cohort file, admission
proposal or baseline is changed. Coverage, separate review and feature admission stay
