### Independence groups

  adjutant-general, and says he was present at Booneville, Carthage and Wilson's Creek. He quotes

  Department, so his history is not independent of those families.

  book is based on the official reports of Lyon, Sigel, Sturgis, Fremont, Price and others, and his

## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** Cole Camp, Dug Springs, the approach marches, the retreat to Rolla, the September 1
  capture of the Fort Scott mules, the Warrensburg advance, the failed relief of Lexington, Thompson's
  railroad raid and the October 17 Fredericktown skirmish, Fremont's advance and occupation of
  Springfield, and the Neosho capture of Conrad's company are context only. Multi-action totals are
  not assigned to one record.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The frozen imported bounds are:

  | Record | US | CS |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | Boonville | 1,700 | blank |
  | Dry Wood Creek | 600 | blank |
  | Lexington | 3,500 | 12,000 |
  | Liberty | 600 | blank |
  | Fredericktown | 2,500 | blank |

  Carthage, Wilson's Creek and Springfield are blank. Fredericktown's force text reads 2,500–3,500
  while the table records 2,500 as both bounds. The Boonville 1,700 matches Lyon's own report; the
  match is not treated as corroboration. Every figure found is recorded with its date, basis and
  scope, and none is adopted:
  - Boonville: Lyon's 1,700 and the Governor's three or four thousand; Snead's two thousand from St.
    Louis, seventeen hundred at Boonville, and Marmaduke's 400–500.
  - Carthage: Sigel's 550 and 400 with two four-gun batteries and 3,500 in sight (4,000 reported);
    Snead's 950 and 125 with seven guns, 2,600 and 1,500 in the State line and 2,000 unarmed.
  - Wilson's Creek: Sturgis's 3,700 against 23,000 and over 20,000; Snead's twelve hundred and
    forty-two hundred in the two columns, 3,500 against 4,200 on Bloody Hill, thirty-one hundred
    against nineteen hundred in the first lines, McIntosh's thousand, Pearce's 1,700, and his six or
    seven thousand for Lyon's army.
  - Dry Wood: Britton's twelve hundred Union cavalry and ten thousand for Price; Lane's "0,000" (OCR)
    to 10,000, about 6,000, and his 800 at Fort Scott.
  - Lexington: Price's 3,500 prisoners; Britton's two thousand, three thousand, Rains's three
    thousand, Parsons's three thousand and 3,000 prisoners.
  - Liberty: Scott's 500 and 70 with a gun crew, and 4,000 and 4,400 for the enemy; Atchison's
    unnumbered regiments and 900 for the enemy.
  - Fredericktown: Plummer's 1,500 and Carlin's 3,000, and 3,500 or 4,000 (6,000 by some wounded)
    for Thompson; Thompson's 3,000, "more than double" and 2,000.
  - Springfield: Zagonyi's 150 (70 after the charges) and 2,100 enemy; Britton's 150 and 180 and at
    least 1,500 enemy; NPS 1,000–1,500.
- **Disputes preserved** (25 claims marked `disputed`: eight force-scope, eight casualty, four
  result, two command-role, and one each for terrain, logistics and information):
  - **Boonville landing.** Lyon's two reports give 6 and about 8 miles below the town; Snead gives 8.
  - **Wilson's Creek result.** Sturgis's perfect rout of the enemy front and withdrawal at leisure
    against Snead's abandonment of the field.
  - **Dry Wood.** The mule capture is placed in the action by NPS and the evening before by Britton;
    Lane claims to have driven back the enemy's advance guard.
  - **Lexington's hemp breastworks.** Price says he caused the bales to be moved; Britton says
    Harris proposed them and asked Price's permission.
  - **Blue Mills.** Contact at 3 p.m. (Scott) against 3:00 am (NPS); an orderly withdrawal (Scott)
    against flight (Atchison).
  - **Fredericktown.** Plummer's total defeat of Thompson against Thompson's "we were victorious,
    though we fell back".
  - **Springfield command.** NPS says White fell ill and handed over to Zagonyi; Zagonyi says White
    was a prisoner before the battle and his command left at the start; Britton has both majors
    deciding the detour.
  - **Frozen against live casualties:** Boonville 81 against 31; Carthage 244 against 118; Wilson's
    Creek 2,330 against 1,781; Lexington 1,874 against 3,600; Liberty 126 against 62. Report and
    history figures differ further in each record (for example Sturgis's 1,235 against Snead's 1,317,
    and Price's 25 killed and 72 wounded against Britton's Union 39 and 120 with 3,000 prisoners).
- **Command roles and ranks.**
  - Sturgis is frozen as Major General at Wilson's Creek; he signs as Major, First Cavalry. Lane is
    frozen as Colonel; the OR heading styles him Brigadier General. Atchison is frozen as General; a
    compiler's footnote says there is no record of his official status. The live page gives Zagonyi
    as Major General, and the NPS description names him Charles against the frozen James. None of
    these ranks is adopted.
  - Governor Jackson ordered Marmaduke out at Boonville over his protest (Snead) and took command in
    person at Carthage. McCulloch accepted command of the combined armies before Wilson's Creek, and
    Sturgis succeeded Lyon and decided to retreat. At Fredericktown, Carlin reported to Plummer for
    orders although he claimed to rank him.
  - No listed commander receives automatic sole credit.
- **Tags.** Two terrain claims are tagged `inherited` (the Carthage prairie ridge and the Wilson's
  Creek ground). The Fredericktown positions are `commander_created` (Thompson placed his line and
  ambush). The Lexington works and the Boonville cover stay `unresolved`; outcomes are `post_outcome`.

The eight null unknowns are the opening strengths. No morale/readiness score, probability, causal
effect or commander ranking is introduced. No model input, frozen baseline or admission proposal is
changed.
