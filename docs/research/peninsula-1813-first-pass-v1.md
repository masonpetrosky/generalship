## What the evidence preserves

- **Populations and times.** La Salud’s initial French 1,500 precede later reinforcements; Spanish 8,000–9,000 is prisoner hearsay. San Sebastián’s opening garrison, assault brigades, siege army and much larger covering army remain separate. Pyrenees opening sectors and terminal Echalar estimates, Ordal’s advance guard and converging columns, and Nive’s infantry comparisons are not interchangeable denominators. Missing own-side local returns remain visible.
- **Internal ambiguity.** Oman calls the Nivelle 82,000 both “infantry” and “infantry and gunners” on one page. The draft retains both labels. Formations, horses, casualties, prisoners and late survivors do not reconstruct opening headcounts.
- **Authority.** Harispe/Suchet, Joseph/Jourdan, Lamarque/Decaen, Rey/Songeon and Graham/Wellington have separate local and superior scopes. Ordal records Adam→Reeves→Carey after wounds. Pampeluna’s Carlos de España versus Bodart’s Don Carlos v. Bourbon label remains unresolved. No equal or continuous command credit is inferred.
- **Posture and works.** Murray’s Castalla works and Soult-ordered river redoubts are explicit commander-created adaptations. Ordal’s ruined Reding trenches and the restored Urugne work have different histories. The latter’s image prints 1793, not OCR 1795. The Nive’s allied 9 December crossing and French 13 December main attack are separate operations. A local countercharge alone does not establish a second main offensive.
- **Chronology and communication.** Lapène prints Pampeluna’s capitulation on 23 October against the frozen 31 October endpoint. San Sebastián’s 31 August storm, 8 September capitulation and 9 September exit remain separate. The Villefranque bridge broke while Ustaritz held; the draft does not infer complete isolation. Reported false messages and missed orders remain source-qualified observations, not invented readiness scores.
