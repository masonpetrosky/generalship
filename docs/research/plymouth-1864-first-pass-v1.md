## Decisions and limits

- **Scope.** The two records are separate actions and neither dossier draws on the other's
  figures. The April 17–20 capture includes the ram's action of April 19. The Bombshell's sinking
  at Plymouth (April 18) and her recapture on May 5 are kept in their own records.
- **Opening strengths remain unknown.** The frozen bounds are blank for both records and the live
  Forces Engaged fields read zero. Every figure is recorded with its date, basis and scope, and none
  is adopted:
  - Plymouth: Wessells's effective men for four regiments, as the garrison "distributed along this
    line" (400, 450, 300, 400; 1,550 by computation; undated in the passage, which sits in his
    narrative of April 17), written from memory four months later and omitting artillery,
    cavalry and the North Carolina companies; the attacking brigades as subsequently ascertained;
    Cooke's 20 seamen and the ships' guns and crew.
  - Albemarle Sound: vessel counts of seven (NPS), eight (Smith's order of attack), nine (frozen
    row) and six plus three (Cooke). Smith's May 24 report names seven vessels entitled to share in
    the Bombshell's capture (the order of attack's eight without the Seymour); a prize-share list is
    not an engagement count.
- **Disputes preserved** (six claims marked `disputed`: two force-scope, two result, two casualty):
  - Plymouth surrender hour: 10 a.m. (Wessells) against 12 m. (Cooke).
  - NPS names the sunk vessel "Smithfield" and the captured work "Fort Comfort"; the reports name
    the Southfield and the Eighty-fifth Redoubt and Battery Worth.
  - The Miami: NPS says she was damaged; Cooke says she escaped, and adds that he has "since
    learned" that her captain was killed and that she sank after reaching Edenton (hearsay).
  - Albemarle Sound: Cooke believed he sank one boat and crippled two; Smith says his shot had no
    perceptible effect and he drove the ram back. Smith's own prisoner counts for the Bombshell
    differ (37 on May 6, 33 on May 24). The frozen result is Inconclusive and the live result
    Indecisive.
  - Casualties:
    - Plymouth: frozen 2,834; live 3,200 (US 2,900; CS 300); the compiled Union return 2,834
      killed, wounded and missing (127 officers, 2,707 men); Wessells's own casualties "did not
      exceed 150"; 850 Confederate killed and wounded relayed from medical officers.
    - Albemarle Sound: frozen 88; live zero; Smith's 5 killed and 26 wounded "in the large vessel"
      before the Sassacus returns; two killed and six wounded named on the Mattabesett.
- **Observation, not reconciliation.** The frozen Plymouth 2,834 equals the printed total of the
  compiled Union return, which includes the captured garrison as missing. The frozen figure's basis
  is not otherwise established, and Confederate losses are not in it.
- **Command roles and ranks.** The frozen row gives Wessells as Colonel (so does the live page); his
  reports are headed and signed Brigadier General. The live page gives Cooke as "Captain James
  Cooke" against the frozen Commander. Cooke calls Hoke Brigadier General. Frozen ranks are kept; the
  others are recorded, not adopted. No listed commander receives automatic sole credit. Flusser,
  Fardella, Lehmann, Beach, Mercer, Elliott and Hopkins are named as attributed actors, not rated.
  The Sassacus and the Miami act only through Smith's report, which does not name their commanders
  in the selected passages.
- **Tags.** The Plymouth works predate the attack, but their effect is not scored; no claim is
  tagged `inherited`. All claims stay `unresolved` or `post_outcome`.
- **Unknowns.** The two null unknowns are the opening strengths.

No morale/readiness score, probability, causal effect or new commander ranking is introduced. The
cohort, both admission proposals and the baseline are unchanged, with zero promoted rows.
