# Naples opening boundaries and membership, v1

Design and primary verification, 2026-10-19. Scope: the same four entries/eight
sides of `third-coalition 1806` used by [ledger design v1](napoleonic-ledgers-v1.md).
The owner accepted the recommended bounded contact, area, availability and ordering
step. Only existing pinned sources were used. This is a measurement specification;
it admits no feature and selects no force, army, person or historical model row.

## Target and common rules

The proposed target is people assigned to combat units, including officers and
artillery crews, physically present in the specified local land-operation area
immediately before its first hostile contact. The same rule and one common
boundary apply to both sides. A source narrative can locate an event relatively
without supplying a synchronized clock or a complete opening population.

- Contact includes hostile fire, assault or forcible displacement of a local
  reconnaissance party or outpost. It does not wait for the main infantry clash.
  Mere sighting, withdrawal without stated hostile action, an investment, a march,
  battery construction or an intended attack is not proof of contact.
- A siege's initial encounter, investment, renewed attack and opening of formal
  batteries are different events. A later phase may be a sensitivity scenario,
  but must never silently replace first contact. The frozen source interval is
  preserved even if the relevant initial encounter predates it.
- Area is declared by named terrain and functional limits before choosing a
  strength. No radius, marching speed or geographic polygon is invented. The
  worked local target excludes distant garrisons, communication posts, expedition
  bases and people still aboard transports. Naval crews are outside this land
  target; personnel demonstrably disembarked into combat units need separate evidence.
- Local reserves and outposts can belong without eventually fighting. A troop
  belongs only if its combat assignment, local presence and availability by the
  proposed boundary are supported. Failure to participate does not establish
  absence or unavailability. A later presence observation alone does not establish
  a late arrival, or prove earlier presence.
- Reported guns are not people. Rank and file do not include officers by default;
  inhabitants are not all combatants. No conversion, imputation, source vote or
  subtraction of unlike populations is permitted.
- Source sequences remain source-specific. The ordering graph records only
  stated or narrative before/after relations; no common clock is inferred across
  accounts. Printed times remain attributed text. Unknown relations remain unknown.

`candidate_component` means a source places a named component locally by a
candidate event under these proposed rules. It is neither an all-arms total nor
an approved historical boundary. `excluded` is conditional on the named scenario
and rule. `unresolved` retains a concrete missing membership/time/area fact.
Observation applicability and the historical membership of a unit are distinct.

## Worked specification

### Campo Tenese, B370c

Proposed area: the local approach after Rotonda through San Martino, Campo Tenese's
plain and flanking heights, ending at the Morano exit. Prior actions around Guaro,
Lauria and Lagonegro, and later pursuit, are separate contexts. The exact outer
limit and the positions of rearward components at contact are unestablished.

Koch p.174 describes the dislodging of Neapolitan reconnaissance horse after the
halt at Rotonda, then the repulse of outposts in the San Martino approach, then
deployment onto the heights. These supply a source-relative contact candidate and
an ordering, not a clock for the whole force. Damas p.395 dates the attack on his
Campo Tenese position to "two o'clock in the afternoon of the 9th March"; that is
not automatically the earlier reconnaissance/outpost boundary. His reported
clock is retained without timezone conversion or reconciliation with Koch.

French scouts and the opposing reconnaissance party supply local component
candidates at Koch's first narrated displacement. Placement of the redoubt
garrison, Verdier's following line and Franceschi's cavalry at that earlier
instant remains unresolved. Koch p.176's nonparticipation statement cannot be
used to remove Franceschi's cavalry from the opening force. Damas's account of
the right wing's separate retreat and intended junction does not put it at Campo
Tenese. The wider-corps and illustrative totals remain as classified in v1.

### Maida, B370d

Proposed area: the local Maida plain, the Lamato's adjoining banks/thickets and
positions of the advancing field columns and local reserve. The beach tower/base,
distant garrisons, transports and the wider expedition are outside this target.
Its exact rear limit and the last column's position at the earliest fire remain
unresolved. The landing fight is earlier expedition context, not silently the
opening of this separately listed field engagement.

Bunbury pp.240–244 orders the daylight march of the 4th, the movement onto the
plain, artillery exchange, the Corsican/20th light-company skirmish, and the main
infantry clash. The artillery exchange is the earliest explicit fire in this
local sequence. The preceding cavalry retirement is not proof of hostile action.
Koch p.189 instead dates the French advance to the 5th after the night of the
3rd–4th. These alternatives are preserved; neither source's date is corrected.

The firing artillery supplies local combat-component candidates, without crew
counts. The marching rank-and-file total remains a partial basis; it cannot be
made all-arms by adding a guessed officer or gun crew allowance. Oswald's reserve
is not excluded for being a reserve, but its exact placement at first fire still
needs evidence. Four Watteville companies and artillerymen left at the tower
(p.240) are outside the declared field area. The 20th's light company (p.243) and
Ross's body reported approaching after the fight was under way (p.246) remain
distinct. That later passage does not give a landing clock or establish the
body's availability at first artillery fire. No later participation is backfilled.

### Gaëta, B371a

Proposed area: the fortress/garrison, local investment line through the faubourg
toward Sérapis, and land siege positions immediately before the place. Distant
Garigliano/communication posts and ship crews are excluded by the proposed local
rule; the camp-to-Terracina group must be split before any local portion can be
included. No complete spatial roster is established.

Koch p.196 describes shots at parlementaires and a subsequent bombardment of
Lacour's lodging, without a complete date on the inspected page. His p.205 dates
the **construction** of a mortar battery to the evening of 4 March; construction
does not establish a first shot. His p.233 describes strong garrison fire during
the preparations preceding the 7 July battery opening. Colletta p.30 calls 7 July
the "prime offese". That wording cannot make 7 July the first hostile contact of
the entire operation. Initial encounter and July battery-opening scenarios stay
separate; neither is installed as a resolved joint opening boundary.

Koch's July local troop and detached-post observations retain their state date.
They cannot describe the initial encounter without a reconstruction. The
Garigliano body is outside the local rule; the camp-to-Terracina group has mixed,
unresolved membership. The July garrison estimates are also later-phase
observations. Colletta's ships do not become extra land personnel.

### Amantea, B379c

Proposed area: the fortress and rock approaches, its landward investment/assault
positions and immediate sea-face approaches on land. Cosenza, the wider mobile
columns, distant insurgent bands and people afloat are outside. The frozen
18 December 1806–6 February 1807 interval is unchanged.

Koch pp.280–282 distinguishes the attempt departing on 3 December and withdrawn
on the 8th, Huard's investment on the 18th, Verdier's departure on the 22nd, the
following day's Reynier column, and later trench/battery/assault work. The
investment is not itself proof of a hostile shot or assault. The trench phase
has no established opening date; "33 days" is not used to manufacture one.
Colletta pp.31–32 gives no date for the initial dawn assault, explicitly places a
subsequent night assault in December, and describes a return after December. These
passages do not independently align his initial attempt with Koch's 3–8 December
attempt or place it before the frozen 18 December start. This refines v1's
"month-only" shorthand: the month belongs explicitly to the night assault, not
to the preceding dawn attack. The old dossier and ledger-design record remain immutable.

Under an explicit **18 December investment scenario**, Huard is a local component
candidate; Verdier's and Reynier's separately departing later columns cannot be
simultaneous opening components there. Their 1,200+2,500+1,000 arithmetic remains
4,700, but fails that scenario's timing rule. At a later formal-attack scenario,
concurrency, unchanged membership and a complete personnel basis are still
unestablished. No source is adjudicated by matching the compiler total. Town
inhabitants, regulars, insurgents and assigned defenders remain separate populations.

## Records, checks and limits

[Records](../data/napoleonic/boundaries-v1/records.json) retain exact citations,
English interpretations, source metadata/raw hashes, page-image references,
events, a source-specific ordering graph, shared area scenarios, membership cases
and all eight sides. All 43 prior strength observations stay indexed with their
old dispositions; these are worked membership cases, not exhaustive new extraction.
The [source snapshot](../data/napoleonic/boundaries-v1/source-snapshot.json) copies
complete existing metadata and parent/facsimile closure. No registry or raw source
changed. The [generated report](../artifacts/napoleonic-boundaries-v1.md) gives exact
coverage and unresolved denominators. All eight selected boundaries/strengths,
army IDs and responsible-person IDs remain null; no complete force pair is emitted.

The old design, examples, verification record, reports and bound code remain
unchanged. The new standalone standard-library module is integrated through
`make check` and `make reproduce`, preserving the earlier CLI/code bindings.
Run `python3 -m generalship.napoleonic_boundaries` for a read-only replay, or add
`--write` to regenerate only this task's JSON/Markdown report. Its own primary
verification binds this design, records, frozen inputs and executed checker/tests.
The shared Makefile is not frozen as research logic; future check integrations
can change it under normal validation without rewriting this verification record.
The general build receipt also includes the new code and data; its legacy output
list does not include this separate report, which the new checker verifies directly.
No prepared dossier packet needs regeneration because its evidence bytes did not change.

Verification checks citation presence, source/image bindings, exact side and
observation coverage, scenario references, acyclic source-specific ordering and
declared membership rules. It cannot establish historical truth, complete troop
availability, source independence or the absence of an earlier unreported encounter.
The work is primary-verified; no separate reviewer, subagent or calibration scoring
ran. The 273/52 development/final reservation and all prior numerical results remain.

Stop here. The next useful bounded step is a person/authority and army-continuity
mapping for these same four entries, exposing which ambiguities actually prevent
a usable force/command pair before expanding ledger extraction. Further source
research must identify a specific admission decision it could change.
