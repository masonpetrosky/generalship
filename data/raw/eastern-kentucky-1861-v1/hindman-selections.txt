## hindman-1861-12-19

No. 4. Report of Brig. Gen. Tlwmas C. Hindman, C. 8. Army, with congratu- latory orders from Major- General Hardee. Headquarters Advance Guard, Cave City, Ky., December 19, 1861. Sir : At 8 a. m. on the 17th instant I moved towards Woodsonville, for the purpose of breaking up the railroad from the vicinity of that place southward. My force consisted of 1,100 infantry, 250 cavalry, and four pieces of artillery. When within 2J miles of Woodsonville, con- cealed from the enemy's view, I halted the column and ordered forward Colonel Terry's Rangers to occupy the heights to my right; left, and front, and Major Phifer's cavalry to watch the crossings of Green River, still farther to my left. These orders having been executed and no force of the enemy or pickets seen, I advanced the column until the right reached the railroad. This brought me within three-quarters of a mile of the river and the enemy, but still concealed, except a small body of cavalry 20 OPERATIONS IN KY., TENN., N. ALA., AND S. \V. VA. [Cmr. XVIL upon the extreme right. Here a company of Eangers was detached to observe the enemy from Eowlett's Knob, which was to my right, across the railroad. A strip of timber bordered the river parallel to the line beld by my cavalry. Fields were between. A body of the enemy's infantry, as skirmishers, moved through the timber by their right on my left. They were tired upon by a small body of my cavalry and re- tired. The firing ceased for about half an hour, and I went in person to select a suitable place for camp, leaving Colonel Terry in command, with in- structions to decoy the enemy upthe hill, where I could use my infantry and artillery with effect and "be out of range of the enemy's batteries. Before returning to the column the fire from the skirmishers recom- menced. The enemy appeared in force upon my right and center. Col- onel Terry, at the head of 75 Eangers, charged about 300, routed and drove them back, but fell mortally wounded. A body of the enemy of about the same size attacked the Eangers, under Captain Ferrill, upon the right of the turnpike, and were repulsed with heavy loss. The en- emy began crossing by regiments and moving around on my right and left flanks. Three companies of Colonel Marin ad uke's (First Arkansas) battalion were thrown out as skirmishers on my left, engaged the enemy's right, and drove them to the river. I now ordered forward Captain Swett's battery and the Second Arkansas Eegiment to support it, hold- ing the Sixth Arkansas Eegiment in reserve. The artillery opened fire upon the enemy in the field adjacent to the railroad and drove them to the banks of the river. Firing now ceased on both sides. The enemy made no further attempt to advance, but knowing that he had already crossed in force, more than doable my own, and had the means of cross- ing additional forces, I withdrew my command by way of the turnpike two miles and a half, and took position to meet the enemy if disposed to advance. There being no indications of any such intention, I returned to my camp here, reaching this place at S p. m. My loss in this affair was as follows : Killed — Colonel Terry and 3 men of his regiment; dangerously wouuded — Lieutenant Morris and 3 men of Texas Eangers ; slightly wounded — Captain Walker and 3 men of Texas Eangers and 2 men of First Arkansas Battalion. I estimate the enemy's loss at 75 killed and left on the ground ; wouuded unknown. I have 7 prisoners ; other prisoners were too badly wounded to be moved, and were left at citizens' houses. The troops under my command who were engaged displayed courage in excess. The others were as steady as veterans. Very respectfully, T. C. HINDMAN, Lieut. D. G. White, Brigadier -General. Acting Assistant Adjutant-General, First Division, &c.
