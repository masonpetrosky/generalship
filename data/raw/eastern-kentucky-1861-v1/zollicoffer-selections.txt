## zollicoffer-1861-09-19

Action at Barboursville, Ky. Report of Brig. Gen. F. K. Zollicoffer, C. 8. Army. BRIGADE HEADQUARTERS, Camp Bucltner, near Cumberland Ford, Ky., Sept. 19, 1861. SIR : On my way here to-day an express overtook me with your order to send two regiments from my command to Camp Trousdale. I imme diately caused orders to be given to Fourteenth Mississippi Regiment, Colonel Baldwin, and Third East Tennessee Regiment, Colonel Lillard, to move to Camp Trousdale, those two regiments being on the line of the railroad and most readily to be brought to the position designated. 1 have now four regiments here and one at Cumberland Gap. I have here one 6-pounder field battery of six guns and four companies of cav alry — eight other cavalry companies on the way. There are now but two infantry regiments left in East Tennessee ; one, the Alabama regi ment, with more than 400 sick. There are five cavalry companies left for that service. An advanced force sent out last night, about 800 strong, entered Barboursville, 18 miles from, here, about daylight, where they found about 300 of the enemy, and a fight ensued, in which we killed 12 and took 2 prisoners. We lost 1 killed, Lieutenant Powell, of Colonel Cum- miugs7 regiment, 1 fatally wounded, and 3 slightly wounded. The enemy tied precipitately. The number of his wounded unknown. Col. J. A. Battle commanded the detachment, making a march of 34 miles and dispersing this detachment of the enemy within a period of twenty hours. He destroyed their encampment, called Camp Andrew Johnson, and captured about 25 arms. Two prisoners had been taken a day or two before, one of whom was bearing a letter from an East Tennessee captain in the Lincoln camp at Hoskins' Cross-Roads to his wife, in which the writer states that the strength of that camp is 15,000 and still rapidly increasing. We .now have a report from the country people that they are 20,000 strong. My only engineer officer understanding military engineering has re signed and gone home. Very respectfully, F. K. ZOLLICOFFEK, Brigadier- General. General A. S. JOHNSTON, Memphis, Tenn.

## zollicoffer-1861-10-24

CAMP FLAT LICK, KNOX COUNTY, October 24, 1861. On the 21st I reached the enemy's intrenched camp, on Eockcastle Hills, a natural fortification, almost inaccessible. Having reconnoitered in force under heavy fire for several hours from heights on the light, left, and in front, I became satisfied that it could not be carried other wise than by immense exposure, if at all. The enemy received large re-enforcements. Our loss was 42 wounded and 11 killed and missing. We captured 21 prisoners, about 100 guns, and 4 horses. The loss of the enemy in killed and wounded unknown. The country is so poor we had exhausted the forage on the road for 15 miles back in twenty-four hours. Our subsistence nearly exhausted. Under these circumstances I deemed it proper the next day to fall bade. Enemy's camp said to be 7,000 strong, with large reserves near at hand. Very respectfully, F. K. ZOLLICOFFEE, Brigadier- Genera I. Lieutenant-Colonel MACKALL, Assistant Adjutant- General, Bowling Green, Ky.

## zollicoffer-1861-10-26

BRIGADE HEADQUARTERS, Camp Buckner, October 26, 1861. SIR : I have information that the enemy were nine regiments at Rock- castle Llills. They are thought to have large re-enforcements close at hand. The country is very poor generally between here and there, par ticularly beyond London. 1 learn that some signs of trouble are again arising in East Tennessee, as the impression increases that the enemy is soon to advance in force. The new levies I learn come in slowly. Could General William E. Caswell, who recently resigned when the Tennessee regiments were transferred, have a commission it would greatly promote the public interest. He has been very efficient in ad vising roe to dispose matters properly in East Tennessee since I have been in Kentucky. He is a true gentleman, of high courage, sound sense, exemplary habits, and of popularity worth much in the present condition of aifairs in East Tennessee. The Log Mountains, between here and Cumberland Gap, will soon, I learn, become almost impassable. The road is now very bad. There is reason to suppose the enemy may advance by way of Jamestown, 120 miles below here, instead of by this route. I have seven cavalry companies watching that route ; no infantry or artillery, two regiments there having been ordered away by General Johnston. If I get news of their approach in that direction I will proceed as rapidly as possible to meet them, and have already collected some stock of commissary stores in that neighborhood. Very respectfully, E. K. ZOLLICOFFEE, Brigadier- General. Acljt. Gen. S. COOPER, Richmond, Va.
