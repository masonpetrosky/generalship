# Worthington 1872: selected opposing testimony as published, pp.137,140

## transcription-note

Selected passages; omitted intervening text is not joined. Whitespace and line-wrap hyphens normalized; typographic quotes/apostrophes normalized. No lexical modernization. These are selections in a polemical publication, not an inspected official transcript. Printed testimony context is 1862; the Crary passage contains (Sept. 5, 1862.), retained literally, but the composition date of this 1872 selection is unknown. Neither catalog attribution nor print authenticates the underlying testimony. No diary claim is extracted.

## p137-sherman-denial

He testified "that no pickets were driven out of the Howell house on Saturday. The house," he says, "which I call the Widow Howell's house, was in a field, near a lane, in Buckland's front, about three-quarters of a mile from his center. Our pickets were a mile in front of that house.

## p140-sharpe-heading

Captain Sharpe, chief picket officer of 46th Ohio volunteer infantry :

## p140-sharpe-expulsion

The pickets were driven from the Howell house on Saturday about 7 a. m. There were no pickets that I know of in front of the Howell house on the Corinth road.

## p140-sharpe-occupation

Question by Colonel W. Were not the enemy in possession of the Howell house all day Saturday?
Answer. They were.

## p140-crary

Lieutenant George F. Crary, 46th Ohio, sworn:
"Question by prisoner. Did you see me on Saturday morning out with the pickets?
"A. I saw you out there early in the morning. We were driven away about 7 a. m., (Sept. 5, 1862.) I heard a rebel drum beat Friday afternoon. The beat appeared to be in more than one regiment."
