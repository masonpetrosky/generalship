At the close of the engagement before Blackburn's Ford I directed General Longstreet to withdraw the First and Seventeenth Eegiments, which had borne the brunt of the action, to a position in reserve, leaving Colonel Early to occupy the field with his brigade and Garland's regi ment. As a part of the history of this engagement I desire to place on record that on the 18th of July not one yard of intrenchments nor one rifle pit sheltered the men at Blackburn's Ford, who, officers and men, with rare exceptions, were on that day for the first time under fire, and who, taking and maintaining every position ordered, cannot be too much commended for their soldierly behavior. Our artillery was manned and officered by those who but yesterday were called from the civil avocations of a busy city.
